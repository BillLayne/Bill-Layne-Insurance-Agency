What North Carolina renters should know

  1. No state law requires renters insurance. A landlord can require it as a condition of the lease and ask for proof before move-in. We can send the certificate the day the policy starts. Full answer: is renters insurance required in NC?
  2. Your landlord's policy covers the building, not you. It does not replace your belongings or defend you if a guest is hurt in your apartment.
  3. Belongings are usually covered for named perils. Fire, theft, windstorm and sudden water discharge are typical. Flood is excluded and needs a separate contents policy. Bed bugs, mold from neglect and a roommate's belongings are generally excluded too. Full list: what renters insurance covers and excludes.
  4. Loss of use pays for somewhere to stay. If a covered loss makes the rental unlivable, the policy pays the extra cost of a hotel or temporary rental. Our loss of use guide walks through it.
  5. Replacement cost beats actual cash value for belongings. One pays to replace your five-year-old laptop with a new one; the other pays what the old one was worth. Ask which one your quote uses.
  6. Students may already have some coverage. Many homeowners policies extend limited coverage to a dependent in a dorm. An off-campus apartment usually needs its own policy. We read the parent policy before we quote the student. See the college student guide.

A few things to know before you move.

Does my landlord’s insurance cover my belongings?+

Your landlord’s policy generally covers the building and landlord-owned property. It does not replace your furniture, clothing, electronics or other personal belongings.

What about roommates?+

A renters policy generally covers the named insured and eligible household members. Unrelated roommates typically need their own policies unless the carrier and policy specifically include them. Review who is insured before assuming everyone is covered. Our roommate guide covers shared policies and couples.

How much coverage do I need, including valuables?+

Estimate the replacement cost of your belongings room by room. Review liability limits, deductibles and additional living expense limits. Jewelry, firearms, collectibles, cameras, money and business equipment may have special limits or need scheduled coverage. Ask whether belongings are covered at replacement cost or actual cash value. Two of our guides help with the math: the renters personal property calculator and how much personal property coverage you really need in NC. Step by step: how much renters insurance do I need?

What does my lease require?+

Review the lease’s liability limit, proof-of-insurance deadline and property manager instructions, including any requested additional insured wording. Ask about pets, roommates and guests. We can help you review the policy against those requirements; coverage starts only when confirmed.

Does renters insurance cover flood or water damage?+

Standard renters policies do not cover outside flood water; flood coverage needs a separate review. Some sudden and accidental plumbing leaks may be covered, depending on the policy. Gradual leaks, maintenance issues and other exclusions still matter.

Can I bundle renters with auto?+

Ask us to compare available auto and renters options. Availability and any multi-policy discount vary by carrier and your circumstances. Review coverage and the overall price before deciding.

What does renters insurance cover?+

It generally helps protect personal belongings, personal liability and additional living expenses after a covered loss. Loss of use covers eligible costs above your normal living expenses, subject to limits and conditions. Coverage depends on the policy, covered cause of loss, limits, deductible and endorsements.

What about my car, work equipment or everyday wear?+

Damage to your vehicle belongs under auto coverage. Belongings stolen from a vehicle depend on the renters policy and type of property. Work-from-home equipment, tools or business inventory may need an endorsement or separate policy. Wear, pest damage, neglect, intentional damage and gradual problems are generally excluded.

Is renters insurance required in North Carolina?+

No state law requires it. Your lease can. Many North Carolina landlords and property managers now require a renters policy with a stated liability limit and ask for proof before handing over keys. The policy is inexpensive relative to what it protects, and we can issue the certificate the day it starts.

Can my landlord require renters insurance and name themselves on it?+

Yes. A lease can make renters insurance a condition of tenancy, set a minimum liability limit, and ask that the landlord or management company be listed as an interested party so they are notified if the policy cancels. That listing does not give them your coverage; it only keeps them informed. Send us the lease clause and we will match it.

Does renters insurance cover a college student in a dorm or an off-campus apartment?+

Many homeowners policies extend limited personal property coverage to a dependent student living in a dorm, often at a reduced limit. An off-campus apartment or house usually falls outside that extension and needs the student’s own renters policy, which also provides liability for the lease. We read the parent policy first so nothing is bought twice.

Does renters insurance cover my belongings if they are stolen from my car?+

Generally yes. Personal property coverage on a renters policy follows your belongings away from the apartment, including a laptop or bag stolen from a vehicle, subject to the deductible and any special limits. The car itself and damage to it are an auto insurance question, which is one reason an auto and renters bundle is worth a look.

What if water from the apartment upstairs damages my things?+

Sudden and accidental water discharge, such as an overflowing tub or a burst line in the unit above, is a covered peril on most renters policies, so your belongings are handled by your own policy and your carrier can pursue the responsible party. Slow leaks, seepage and mold from neglect are generally excluded, and flood from outside the building needs a separate flood contents policy.

More help choosing your limits +

Add up your stuff: Walk room by room, including closets, garages and storage. Estimate what replacing your belongings would cost today.

Review liability: Consider your lease requirements and potential responsibility for injury or property damage.

Check special limits: Valuables and business property may have sub-limits or require an endorsement.

Choose a deductible: Review an amount you could afford after a covered loss, along with replacement cost versus actual cash value coverage.

Common scenarios: Fire, theft, a guest injury or sudden water damage can raise different coverage questions. The cause of loss and policy wording matter.
