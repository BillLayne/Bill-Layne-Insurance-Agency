Renters insurance for

Living in a dorm: the parents' policy usually reaches

Most homeowners policies extend personal property coverage to a dependent student who lived at home before leaving for school, is enrolled full-time, and is under the policy's age limit. The coverage applies while the student lives in university housing, at a reduced limit for property kept away from the home, and the family's deductible applies.

That means a stolen laptop or a fire in a residence hall is often a claim on the parents' policy. Two things to check before relying on it: the away-from-home limit, which can be low compared with a modern student's electronics, and the deductible, which can be higher than the value of what was lost. Liability for a dorm incident generally follows the parents' policy as well.

Off campus: the student usually needs a policy

Once a student signs a lease on an apartment or house, two things change. The lease almost always requires a renters policy with a stated liability limit, and the parents' homeowners carrier may no longer treat the student as a resident of the parents' household, which is the hinge that the away-from-home extension turns on. The further the student is from "temporarily away at school" and the closer to "lives in Boone now," the less the parents' policy applies.

A student renters policy solves both problems. It names the student, lists the rental address, meets the lease's liability requirement, covers belongings at replacement cost, and includes loss of use if the apartment becomes unlivable. We write them for one year and move the address each August when the student moves.

Roommates, leases and who is actually covered

  • A renters policy covers the named insured and resident relatives. An unrelated roommate is not covered by your policy and needs their own, even on a joint lease.
  • Some carriers will add a roommate as a second named insured, which splits one premium but also shares one deductible and one claims history. We usually recommend separate policies for unrelated roommates. Our roommate guide walks through the trade-offs.
  • If the lease is joint and several, each tenant can be held responsible for the whole lease, which is one more reason each person wants their own liability coverage.

What a student actually needs

  • Personal property at replacement cost, with the limit set from a quick inventory: laptop, phone, tablet, bike, instruments, clothes, textbooks and furniture add up faster than students expect. The renters personal property calculator takes five minutes.
  • Liability at or above the lease requirement, which also protects the parents' assets if the student is sued.
  • Loss of use, so a mid-semester fire does not mean a hotel bill on a student budget.
  • Scheduled items for anything that exceeds a special limit, such as a high-end camera, an instrument or an engagement ring.
  • Bike and electronics theft away from the apartment are covered by the personal property section on most forms, subject to the deductible.

Student renters insurance questions

Does my parents' homeowners insurance cover my dorm room?+

Usually yes, at a reduced away-from-home limit and subject to the parents' deductible, as long as the student is a dependent who was a resident of the household and is enrolled full-time. Check the limit against the value of the electronics in the room.

Do I need renters insurance for an off-campus apartment in North Carolina?+

Almost always. The lease will usually require it with a stated liability limit, and the parents' policy may no longer treat an off-campus tenant as a household member. A student renters policy is inexpensive and solves both problems.

Can my roommate and I share one renters policy?+

Some carriers allow an unrelated roommate as a second named insured, but you then share one deductible and one claims history. For unrelated roommates we usually recommend separate policies. Resident relatives, such as siblings, are covered automatically.

Does renters insurance cover my laptop if it is stolen on campus or from my car?+

Generally yes. Personal property coverage follows your belongings away from the apartment, including theft from a car or a library, subject to the deductible and any special limit for electronics.

Who should be the named insured, the student or the parent?+

The student, since the student is the tenant on the lease and the one who needs the liability coverage. A parent can pay the premium and be the contact. Listing the parent instead can leave the lease requirement unmet.

What happens to the policy over the summer or when I move apartments?+

The policy stays in force; we change the address when you move and can add a storage unit if belongings are stored for the summer. If you move home for good, the policy can be cancelled with a pro-rated refund.
