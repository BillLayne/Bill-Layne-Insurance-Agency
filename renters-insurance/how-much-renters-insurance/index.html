How much renters
insurance do I need?

Three numbers decide the policy: the personal property limit, the liability limit and the deductible. Here is how to set each one in about fifteen minutes, with the inventory tool and the calculator we use in the office.

Step 1: the personal property limit

Walk the apartment room by room and estimate what it would cost to replace everything today, not what you paid. Closets, the kitchen, the bathroom cabinet, the bike on the balcony and the contents of a storage unit all count. People routinely guess half of the real number, because the small things add up: a kitchen alone is often more than the sofa.

Two tools make it quick. The renters personal property calculator walks through categories with typical ranges, and the room-by-room inventory tool builds a list from photos you can keep for a claim. Round the total up, not down; the premium difference between limits is small and the gap after a fire is not.

Then choose replacement cost rather than actual cash value. Replacement cost pays to buy new; actual cash value subtracts depreciation, so a five-year-old laptop pays out at a five-year-old laptop's value. The upgrade is inexpensive and it is the single setting that most changes what a claim check looks like.

Step 2: the liability limit

Start with the lease. Most North Carolina leases that require renters insurance state a minimum liability limit, and the policy has to meet it. Then look past the lease: liability pays for injury or damage you cause to others, including a fire that spreads to neighboring units, and it pays your legal defense. The right limit depends on what you have to lose, meaning savings, income and future earnings, not on the size of the apartment.

Carriers offer a few liability tiers and the step from the lowest to the next one usually costs very little per month. If you have meaningful assets, a dog, or host often, choose the higher tier, and ask about an umbrella policy that sits over both renters and auto.

Step 3: the deductible

The deductible is what you pay before the policy pays on a belongings claim. A higher deductible lowers the premium, but only choose a number you could pay next week without borrowing. For most renters the sensible range is the middle of the carrier's options; the premium saving from the highest deductible rarely justifies the pain of a claim you cannot afford to open.

Check the special limits before you finish

Standard forms cap jewelry, firearms, cash, silverware, business property and sometimes electronics at amounts below their real value. If one item or category exceeds the cap, schedule it on the policy. Scheduling removes the cap and typically removes the deductible for that item. Our guide to what renters insurance covers lists the categories.

A fifteen-minute worksheet

  1. Inventory: photograph each room, including inside closets and drawers, and total the replacement cost.
  2. Round the total up to the next limit the carrier offers.
  3. Choose replacement cost on contents.
  4. Read the lease's insurance clause and note the liability minimum.
  5. Choose the liability tier at or above the lease, higher if you have assets or a dog.
  6. Pick a deductible you could pay next week.
  7. List anything that might exceed a special limit.
  8. Send the list and the lease clause to us; we quote four carriers against the same numbers so the comparison is apples to apples. Our what we need to quote you page has the full checklist.

Questions about renters limits

How much personal property coverage should a renter carry?+

Enough to replace everything you own at today's prices, which most people underestimate. Walk every room with the inventory tool or the calculator, total it, and round up to the next limit the carrier offers.

Should I choose replacement cost or actual cash value for my belongings?+

Replacement cost. It pays to buy new rather than paying the depreciated value of what you lost. The premium difference is small and the claim difference is large.

How much liability coverage do I need on a renters policy?+

At least the minimum your lease requires, and higher if you have savings, income, a dog or guests often. The step up between tiers usually costs very little, and an umbrella policy can extend it further.

What deductible should I pick on renters insurance?+

One you could pay next week without borrowing. A higher deductible lowers the premium, but the saving from the highest option rarely justifies a claim you cannot afford to open.

Do I need to list every item I own to get renters insurance?+

No. You choose a total personal property limit. A written or photo inventory is for your own records and speeds up a claim; only high-value items that exceed a special limit need to be scheduled individually.

Does renters insurance cover items in a storage unit?+

Generally yes, as personal property away from the residence, often at a reduced limit. Tell us the unit location and contents so the limit is set correctly.
