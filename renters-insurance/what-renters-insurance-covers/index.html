What renters insurance
covers, and what it does not.

A renters policy has four parts, pays on a specific list of causes, and excludes a handful of things people assume are covered. Here is the whole picture in plain English, from the HO-4 form most North Carolina carriers use.

The four parts of a renters policy

  1. Personal property. Your belongings, anywhere in the world, up to the limit you choose. Furniture, clothing, electronics, kitchenware, bikes, tools and the contents of a storage unit, subject to special limits on certain categories.
  2. Personal liability. Pays what you are legally responsible for if you injure someone or damage their property, and pays the lawyer to defend you. The fire that spreads to the unit next door, the dog bite in the hallway, the guest who falls on your stairs.
  3. Medical payments to others. Small, no-fault payments for a guest's medical bills so a minor injury does not become a lawsuit.
  4. Loss of use. The additional cost of living somewhere else while a covered loss is repaired: a hotel, a short-term rental, extra mileage and meals above what you normally spend. Our loss of use guide covers the details.

The causes of loss that trigger a belongings claim

The standard renters form covers personal property on a named-peril basis. If the cause is on the list, the claim is covered; if it is not, it is not. The list on the common form:

  • Fire or lightning
  • Windstorm or hail
  • Explosion
  • Riot or civil commotion
  • Aircraft
  • Vehicles
  • Smoke
  • Vandalism or malicious mischief
  • Theft
  • Falling objects
  • Weight of ice, snow or sleet
  • Accidental discharge or overflow of water or steam
  • Sudden tearing apart, cracking or bulging of a heating, cooling or water system
  • Freezing of plumbing or appliances
  • Sudden, accidental damage from artificially generated electrical current
  • Volcanic eruption

Some carriers offer an "open-peril" or "special form" upgrade for belongings, which flips the logic: everything is covered unless it is specifically excluded. It costs a little more and pays for things like dropping your phone in the sink. Ask us whether the carrier you are considering offers it.

What renters insurance does not cover

  • Flood from outside the building, including rising water and storm surge. Needs a separate flood contents policy, which matters in Wilmington, the Outer Banks and along the rivers in the Piedmont.
  • Earthquake and earth movement, unless added by endorsement.
  • Bed bugs, insects, rodents and vermin, and the cost of extermination.
  • Mold from neglect or long-term leaks. Sudden water damage is covered; a slow drip under the sink for six months is not.
  • A roommate's belongings. Your policy covers you and resident relatives. Unrelated roommates need their own policy. See our roommate guide.
  • Your car and anything attached to it. Theft of belongings from inside the car is a renters claim; the car itself is an auto claim.
  • Business property above a small limit, and liability from a home business. Work-from-home equipment and inventory may need an endorsement.
  • Intentional acts and damage from wear, deterioration or neglect.

Special limits that surprise people

Even within covered property, the form caps certain categories at amounts that are often below their real value: cash, jewelry and watches, firearms, silverware, securities, business property and sometimes electronics. If you own an engagement ring, a nice camera, a firearm collection or a guitar you would miss, ask about scheduling the item on the policy. It removes the cap and usually removes the deductible for that item. The numbers vary by carrier, so send us a short list and we will match it to the right form.

Add-ons worth asking about

  • Replacement cost on contents instead of actual cash value, so a five-year-old sofa is replaced with a new one rather than paid at its used value. Our how much do I need guide explains the choice.
  • Water backup from a sewer or drain, which the base form excludes.
  • Identity theft expense coverage.
  • Scheduled personal property for valuables.
  • Pet liability confirmation, since some carriers restrict certain breeds and some exclude animal liability entirely.
  • An umbrella policy if you have assets or income to protect beyond the renters liability limit.

Coverage questions renters ask

Does renters insurance cover water damage?+

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, an overflowing tub or a leak from the unit above, is covered on the standard form. Flood from outside the building, sewer backup unless added, and slow leaks or mold from neglect are not.

Does renters insurance cover theft outside my apartment?+

Yes. Personal property coverage follows your belongings worldwide, including theft from your car, your office or a hotel, subject to the deductible and any special limits on categories such as jewelry or electronics.

Does renters insurance cover bed bugs?+

No. Insect and vermin damage and extermination costs are excluded on standard renters forms in North Carolina.

Does renters insurance cover my roommate's belongings?+

No. The policy covers the named insured and resident relatives. An unrelated roommate needs their own policy, or must be added as a named insured where the carrier allows it.

Does renters insurance cover a dog bite?+

Usually yes under personal liability, including a bite that happens away from the apartment, but some carriers restrict certain breeds or exclude animal liability. Tell us about the dog when you quote so the right carrier is chosen.

What is the difference between named-peril and open-peril renters coverage?+

Named-peril covers belongings only for the causes listed in the policy, such as fire, theft and windstorm. Open-peril, sometimes called special form, covers any cause not specifically excluded, including accidental drops and spills. Open-peril costs a little more and is worth asking about.
