Roommates and
renters insurance.

A renters policy covers the people named on it and their resident relatives. A roommate is neither. Here is who is covered, when sharing a policy is allowed, why we usually recommend separate ones, and how married and unmarried couples are handled.

Who a renters policy covers

The policy defines an insured as the named insured, the named insured's spouse if a resident of the household, and relatives who live in the household, plus other people under a certain age in the care of an insured. A roommate who is not related to you and not married to you does not fit any of those. Their belongings are not covered by your policy, and your policy does not defend them if they are sued.

Roommates who are related, such as siblings or cousins sharing a house in Chapel Hill, are covered as resident relatives on one policy.

Why separate policies usually win

  • One deductible per claim. On a shared policy a fire that destroys both bedrooms is one claim with one deductible, which sounds good until you realize one limit also has to cover both people's belongings.
  • One claims history. A claim your roommate files follows you to your next policy, and vice versa.
  • One cancellation. If the roommate stops paying their half, the whole policy can lapse, and the lease clause applies to both of you.
  • Moving out is messy. When one roommate leaves, the policy has to be rewritten, and the person who stays may be uninsured for a stretch.
  • Separate policies are inexpensive. The second policy typically costs about what the increase in limits on a shared one would, without any of the entanglement.

When sharing a policy is allowed

Some carriers allow an unrelated roommate to be added as a second named insured. Each carrier sets its own rules, and some decline it entirely. If you choose that route, both names appear on the declarations page, both are insured for liability, and both sets of belongings count against one personal property limit. Set the limit for both inventories combined, not one. We will tell you which of our four renters carriers permits it before you decide.

Married couples, partners and significant others

  • Married couples are covered on one policy automatically, since a resident spouse is an insured by definition.
  • Unmarried partners are treated like roommates on most forms. Many carriers will add a domestic partner as a named insured; others require a separate policy. Tell us when you quote so the right carrier is chosen.
  • Engaged couples moving in together often buy one policy in both names and schedule the ring. Ask about the engagement ring's special limit.

Joint leases and liability

Most North Carolina roommate leases are joint and several, meaning the landlord can hold any one tenant responsible for the entire lease, including damage caused by a roommate or a roommate's guest. Your renters liability coverage responds when you are legally responsible for damage, but it does not pay for a roommate's negligence; that is their policy's job. One more reason each adult on the lease should carry their own. The lease requirement itself is covered in our guide to whether renters insurance is required in NC.

Roommate questions

Does my renters insurance cover my roommate?+

No, unless the roommate is a resident relative or is added as a named insured where the carrier allows it. An unrelated roommate's belongings and liability are not covered by your policy.

Can two roommates share one renters policy in North Carolina?+

Sometimes. Some carriers allow an unrelated roommate as a second named insured; others require separate policies. Sharing means one personal property limit, one deductible and one claims history for both people, which is why we usually recommend separate policies.

Is my girlfriend or boyfriend covered on my renters policy?+

Not automatically. An unmarried partner is treated like a roommate on most forms. Many carriers will add a domestic partner as a named insured; tell us when you quote.

If my roommate starts a fire, does my renters insurance pay for my things?+

Yes. Your personal property coverage pays for your belongings regardless of who caused a covered loss, and your carrier may pursue the roommate or their insurer afterward. Your roommate's own belongings are their policy's responsibility.

What happens to a shared renters policy when a roommate moves out?+

The policy has to be rewritten to remove the departing named insured and reset the limits, and the person leaving needs a new policy at the new address. With separate policies, each person simply changes their own address.
