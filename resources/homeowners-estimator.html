Why Insurance Rebuilding Cost Differs From Zillow or Tax Value

One of the most common questions North Carolina homeowners ask is why their Coverage A limit doesn't match their real-estate price or county tax assessment.

Real Estate / Tax Value

What Your Home & Land Would Sell For

Market price is driven by land acreage, neighborhood demand, school districts, and comparable sales—not what a contractor charges to build a single custom house after a fire.

  • Includes Land & Lot Value: Yes (Land doesn't burn)
  • Includes Demolition & Debris Removal: No
  • Reflects Single-Lot Custom Rebuild Labor: No
  • Accounts for 2026 NC Building Code Upgrades: No
Insurance Replacement Cost (Coverage A)

What It Costs to Rebuild From the Ground Up

If a fire or tornado destroys your home, a builder has to haul away debris, order materials at current retail prices, and rebuild one structure to modern North Carolina building codes.

  • Debris Removal & Site Prep: Included (Up to 5%–10%)
  • 2026 NC Skilled Trades & Lumber: $205 – $285+ / sq ft
  • Modern Building Code Compliance: Ordinance or Law Coverage
  • Protection Against Underinsurance: Avoids the 80% Coinsurance Penalty
Coverage Clarity

The 6 Core Protections Inside a North Carolina Homeowners Policy

Every standard NC homeowners policy (HO-3 or HE-7) is organized into six sections—Coverage A through Coverage F.

Coverage A • Dwelling

Your Home's Structure

Covers the physical house, foundation, roof, attached garage, built-in appliances, HVAC, plumbing, and permanent decking against fire, wind, hail, lightning, and falling trees.

Coverage B • Other Structures

Detached Buildings & Fences

Automatically set at 10% of Coverage A—and can be increased if you have a detached garage, workshop, guest cottage, pole barn, or extensive fencing.

Coverage C • Personal Property

Everything Inside Your Home

Covers furniture, clothes, kitchenware, tools, and electronics. Pro tip: Always request Replacement Cost on contents so you aren't paid depreciated garage-sale value!

Coverage D • Loss of Use

Temporary Living Expenses

If a covered fire or tree strike makes your home unlivable, Coverage D (typically 20% of Coverage A) pays for a rental house, hotel, and extra meal costs while you rebuild.

Coverage E • Personal Liability

Lawsuit & Injury Protection

Protects your savings and home equity ($100,000 to $500,000+) if a visitor is injured on your property or you accidentally damage someone else's property.

Coverage F • Medical Payments

No-Fault Guest Medical

Pays smaller medical bills ($1,000 to $5,000) if a neighbor or guest slips on your steps—regardless of fault—helping prevent minor accidents from turning into lawsuits.

Frequently Asked Questions

NC Homeowners Coverage Questions Answered

What happens if my Dwelling coverage is less than 80% of my rebuilding cost?

Under standard homeowners insurance rules, if your Coverage A limit falls below 80% of your home's full replacement cost at the time of a loss, the insurer can apply a coinsurance penalty—meaning even a partial claim (like a $40,000 kitchen fire) may only be partially reimbursed. Reviewing your rebuilding estimate every 2–3 years keeps you safely above that threshold.

Does standard NC homeowners insurance cover water backup or underground pipes?

A sudden burst pipe inside your walls is typically covered, but water backing up through a sewer/drain or a broken underground water/sewer line in your yard is excluded unless you add Water Backup and Service Line Coverage endorsements. Both are inexpensive ($35–$75/year) and can save you $10,000+ in excavation and cleanup costs.

How does roof age affect homeowners insurance in North Carolina?

Once a shingle roof reaches 15 to 20 years old, some carriers switch roof wind/hail claims from Replacement Cost to a Roof Payment Schedule (Actual Cash Value). Because we represent multiple NC carriers—including NC Grange Mutual, Travelers, Foremost, and Alamance Farmers—we can help you compare how each carrier treats your roof before you buy.
