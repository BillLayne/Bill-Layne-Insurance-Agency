Quick Answer

Effective October 1, 2026, North Carolina DMV switched to an all-electronic vehicle registration system and stopped issuing physical plate stickers and automatically mailed registration cards. To get a free PDF copy of your registration card anytime, visit the official NCDMV Digital Vehicle Registration portal on ncdot.gov and enter your license plate number plus the last 5 digits of your vehicle title number.

Step 1: Grab These 2 Numbers Before You Click

Check off both items below so you can pull up your PDF on the first try. We do not collect or store your plate or title number on this page.

Don't know where to find your NC vehicle title number?
  • Registration renewal notice: Look near the top of your paper or emailed NCDMV tax & tag renewal notice for the Title Number.
  • Paper NC title: If you own your vehicle outright, the full title number is printed in the upper section of your pink/blue NC Certificate of Title — use only the last 5 digits.
  • MyDMV online account: If your vehicle is financed and the lender holds the title, sign in to your official MyDMV account to view your vehicle's title number.

Step 3: Save Your PDF to Your Phone (or Print Free at Home)

North Carolina law enforcement verifies registration electronically, so a paper copy is no longer required — but keeping a saved PDF handy makes dealership visits, inspections, and out-of-state travel easy.

  1. After NCDMV generates your registration PDF in Safari, tap the Share icon (square with upward arrow) at the bottom of your screen.
  2. Tap Save to Files, choose On My iPhone (so it opens even with zero cell signal), and name it NC Registration + Vehicle Name.
  3. Optional: Take a quick screenshot of the PDF and add it to a dedicated Glovebox album in Photos alongside your auto insurance ID card.
  1. When the NCDMV PDF downloads in Chrome, open your Files app and check the Downloads folder.
  2. Tap the three-dot menu next to the file and choose Rename (for example, NC Registration - Honda) or upload it to Google Drive and turn on Make available offline.
  3. Keep your auto insurance ID card in the same folder so both documents are ready in seconds.
  1. Open the downloaded PDF on your computer or phone and select Print — printing your own copy from NCDMV's PDF is $0 (free).
  2. Prefer an official agency-printed card? You can still request a printed copy at a License Plate Agency or online through NCDMV for a $5 fee.
  3. Fold your printed copy and store it in your glovebox with your current North Carolina auto insurance card.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why didn't I get a North Carolina license plate sticker in the mail?

Effective October 1, 2026, the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles transitioned to an all-electronic vehicle registration system. NCDMV no longer issues physical license plate renewal stickers or automatically mails printed registration cards.

Where do I download a free PDF of my NC vehicle registration?

You can download a free official PDF anytime through the NCDMV Digital Vehicle Registration portal on ncdot.gov by entering your North Carolina license plate number and the last five digits of your vehicle title number.

Where do I find the last five digits of my NC vehicle title number?

Your North Carolina title number is printed on your registration renewal notice, on your paper vehicle title (if you own the car outright), and inside your online MyDMV account. You only need the final five digits.

Do I still have to keep a paper registration card in my glovebox in NC?

North Carolina law enforcement verifies registration status electronically, so carrying a paper registration card is no longer required. However, saving the free PDF to your phone or printing a copy at home is helpful for out-of-state travel, dealerships, or insurance updates. A License Plate Agency can also print a copy for a small state fee.

Why is NCDMV blocking my registration renewal for insurance?

North Carolina requires continuous liability insurance on every registered vehicle. If NCDMV does not have an active electronic insurance verification (Form FS-1) on file or sent a lapse notice, you must respond by the date printed on your notice (thirty days as of October 1, 2026) and ensure active NC coverage is reported before renewing.
