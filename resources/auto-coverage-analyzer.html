Plain-English Coverage Guide

Understand Every Line on Your NC Auto Policy

No insurance jargon—here is what each part of your North Carolina auto policy actually pays for.

Required in NC

Bodily Injury & Property Damage Liability

Pays for other people's medical bills, lost wages, legal defense, and vehicle repairs if you cause an accident. Never pays for your own car.

Required in NC

Uninsured / Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM)

Protects you and your passengers if you're hit by an uninsured driver, a hit-and-run driver, or someone whose limits are too low to cover your hospital bills.

Required if Financed

Collision Coverage

Pays to repair or replace your vehicle if you hit another car, a guardrail, a tree, or roll over—regardless of who was at fault, minus your deductible.

Required if Financed

Comprehensive (Other Than Collision)

Covers events out of your control: North Carolina deer strikes, falling tree limbs, hail, theft, vandalism, flood, and windshield glass breakage.

Highly Recommended

Medical Payments (MedPay)

Pays immediate ambulance, X-ray, and ER copays ($1,000 to $10,000 per person) for you and your family after a crash—with $0 deductible and regardless of fault.

Optional Add-Ons

Rental Car, Roadside & Loan/Lease Gap

Rental Reimbursement pays for a rental car while yours is in the body shop. Gap coverage pays the difference if a financed car is totaled for less than you owe.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common NC Auto Coverage Questions

What changed with North Carolina's 50/100/50 auto insurance law?

Effective July 1, 2025, North Carolina increased its mandatory minimum liability limits from 30/60/25 to $50,000 Bodily Injury per person / $100,000 Bodily Injury per accident / $50,000 Property Damage. Uninsured and Underinsured Motorist (UM/UIM) coverage is also included on all NC policies. Even with this increase, we often recommend 100/300/100 for homeowners and commuters because the price difference is small compared to the added protection.

Does hitting a deer in North Carolina count as Collision or Comprehensive?

Hitting an animal (like a deer) is covered under Comprehensive coverage, not Collision—and in North Carolina, a Comprehensive animal-strike claim does not give you SDIP insurance points! However, if you swerve to miss a deer and hit a tree or guardrail, that is treated as an at-fault Collision claim.

Do I need special coverage if I use my car for business or client visits?

If you drive to multiple job sites, visit clients, carry tools, or make deliveries, be sure to select Business Use on your policy or ask us about a commercial auto quote. Standard personal policies exclude food/package delivery apps unless specifically endorsed.
