WHY YOU GOT THIS FORM

Your insurance company wants to know about your wood heat.

A wood stove, fireplace insert or wood furnace makes a home warm. It also needs to be set up safely. The company asks a few questions so it can see that yours is. That's all this form is.

TYPE

What kind of wood heater do you have?

Look at the four pictures. Find the one that looks most like yours. Check that one box on the form.

Most common today: a modern stove with a glass door and tight seals. If it stands on its own, it still goes under the furnace line for "tight-fitting doors." If it sits in a fireplace, it is an insert. When in doubt, send us a photo.

3

CONSTRUCTION

What is it made of?

Check one box. Look at the outside of the stove and inside the door.

  • Sheet metal: smooth, flat steel panels. Lighter. Sounds thin if you tap it.
  • Cast iron: thick, heavy, often with raised patterns. Very heavy to move.
  • Brick lined: tan bricks stacked inside the firebox. A steel or cast iron stove can also be brick lined.

Any cracks or broken parts? Look at the door, glass, legs and body. Check Yes or No. If yes, write what is broken in the line below it.

Be honest here. A crack is not a deal breaker. It just needs a note.

4

FUEL AND USE

What do you burn, and how much do you use it?

Fuel

Check what you put in it.

  • Wood
  • Coal
  • Other (write it in)

Principal use

Check the one that fits best.

  • Primary heat It heats the house most of the winter. More than 50 days a year. Send a photo of the stove.
  • Supplemental heat You also have a furnace or heat pump. Less than 50 days a year.
  • Cooking A wood cook stove.

Easy way to decide: Is the stove your main heat on a cold January night? That's primary. Is it just for cozy weekends? That's supplemental.

5

INSTALLED AND INSPECTED

When was it put in, and who checked it?

Year installed

Write the year the stove was put in. A best guess is fine. Then check who did it: Owner, Contractor, or Other. Write the name if it was a company.

Date inspected

If a building inspector or the fire department looked at it, write that date and check who. If nobody has, write "none". Don't make one up.

Never inspected? That's common. Many local fire departments will do a free look. Call us and we can point you to the right person.

6

INSTALLATION

Three safety measurements

Grab the tape measure. Answer Yes or No to each one. The picture shows exactly where to measure.

  1. 1
    Space from walls

    Measure from the stove to the nearest wall or furniture. Is it 36 inches or more? If there is a metal or brick shield on the wall with a 1-inch gap behind it, 18 inches is enough.

    Yes if it is far enough. No if it is closer.
  2. 2
    Floor protection

    Is the stove sitting on a pad, tile, brick or stone that will not burn? Does the pad stick out 18 inches in front of the door and 6 inches past the sides and back?

    Yes if it does. No if it sits on carpet or bare wood.
  3. 3
    Air space under the stove

    Look under the stove. Is there at least 4 inches of open air between the floor pad and the bottom of the stove?

    Yes if the legs are tall enough. No if it sits flat.

A "No" is okay. Write it down honestly. We may just ask for a photo or a small fix.

7

CHIMNEY AND FLUE

Where does the smoke go?

Type of chimney

  • Masonry: a brick or block chimney built into the house.
  • UL listed metal chimney: a round metal pipe that goes up through the roof or out the wall. It has a UL sticker on it.

Date last cleaned and date last inspected: write the month and year if you know. If you don't, write "unknown."

How are ashes disposed of? Say where they go. Good answers: "metal bucket with a lid, kept outside" or "cooled ashes go to the garden."

Chimney flue

The flue is the pipe from the stove to the chimney.

  • Passes through a non-combustible thimble collar? Where the pipe goes into the wall or ceiling, is there a metal or ceramic ring around it? Yes or No.
  • More than two bends? Count the elbows in the pipe. Two or fewer is No. Three or more is Yes.

Not sure what a thimble is? Take a photo of where the pipe meets the wall and send it to us. We'll figure it out together.
