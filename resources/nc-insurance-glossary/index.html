NC Insurance Glossary: Auto & Home Terms Explained

45 insurance words you'll see on a quote, a dec page or an NCDMV letter, explained in plain English for North Carolina, with links to our full guides.

General information, not legal advice. Your policy's wording decides what is covered.

#ABCDEFGHILMNPRSTUW

#

50/100/50 Auto
North Carolina's minimum auto liability limits: $50,000 for one person's injuries, $100,000 for all injuries in one accident, and $50,000 for damage to other people's property. These are the least you can carry, not a recommendation.
NC minimum limits →

A

Actual cash value (ACV) Buying & billing
What damaged property was worth right before the loss: replacement cost minus wear and age. An ACV settlement on an older roof or car pays less than the cost of a brand-new one.
ACV roof claims →

B

Bodily injury liability Auto
Pays for injuries you cause to other people in a crash, such as medical bills and lost wages, and pays for your legal defense. It never pays for your own injuries.

C

CLUE report Buying & billing
A claims-history report (Comprehensive Loss Underwriting Exchange) that insurers check when you apply. It lists past auto or home claims tied to you or to a property, so a home's old claims can follow it to a new owner.
CLUE reports when you buy a home →
Collision coverage Auto
Pays to repair or replace your car after it hits another vehicle or an object, or rolls over, no matter who is at fault, minus your deductible. Lenders usually require it on a financed or leased car.
Collision coverage in NC →
Comprehensive coverage Auto
Pays for damage to your car from something other than a collision: theft, fire, hail, falling trees, flood, vandalism and hitting a deer. Also called other-than-collision.
Comprehensive vs. collision →
Consent to rate Home
A North Carolina rule that lets an insurer charge more than the approved rate. For a home, the company must print a notice at the front of your policy showing the approved premium and its own (N.C.G.S. 58-36-30). You no longer sign a consent form, and you can shop elsewhere.
Consent to rate explained →
Contributory negligence NC DMV & law
North Carolina is one of the few states that can bar you from collecting from the other driver if you were even slightly at fault for the crash. There are exceptions. It is one reason your own coverages, like collision and MedPay, matter here.
Coverage A: Dwelling Home
The part of a home policy that pays to repair or rebuild the house itself and attached structures like a garage or deck. It should match the cost to rebuild, not the market price of the home.
NC HO-3 coverage guide →
Coverage B: Other structures Home
Pays for structures on your property that are not attached to the house, such as a detached garage, shed, barn or fence. It is usually a percentage of Coverage A.
Coverage C: Personal property Home
Pays for your belongings: furniture, clothes, electronics and tools. Some items, like jewelry and firearms, have lower limits unless you add them to the policy.
How much personal property coverage →
Coverage D: Loss of use Home
Pays extra living costs, like a hotel and meals, while your home cannot be lived in after a covered loss.
Loss of use coverage →
Coverage E: Personal liability Home
Pays if you are legally responsible for someone else's injury or property damage, at your home or away from it, and pays for your legal defense.
Coverage F: Medical payments to others Home
Pays small medical bills for a guest hurt on your property, no matter who is at fault. It does not cover you or the people who live with you.

D

Declarations page (dec page) Buying & billing
The summary page of your policy: who and what is insured, the coverages and limits, deductibles, discounts and premium. It is the page to send us when you want a quote compared.
Upload your dec page →
Deductible Buying & billing
The part of a covered claim you pay before insurance pays the rest. A higher deductible usually lowers the premium but means more out of pocket at claim time.
NC deductible guide →
DL-123 NC DMV & law
An insurance certificate from your insurer that NCDMV accepts as proof of liability insurance for certain license transactions, such as getting a license back. NCDMV also accepts some other proof documents.
NC DL-123 guide →
Dwelling fire policy (DP-1, DP-3) Home
The policy for a home you rent out to someone else, also called landlord insurance. It covers the building and can add liability and lost rent; it does not cover the tenant's belongings.
Landlord insurance in NC →

E

Endorsement Buying & billing
A change or add-on to a policy, such as adding water backup coverage to a home policy or a new car to an auto policy. Also called a rider.
Equipment breakdown Home
An optional home coverage for sudden mechanical or electrical breakdown of systems and appliances, like an HVAC unit or water heater, which a standard home policy does not cover.
Equipment breakdown guide →

F

Flood insurance Home
A separate policy for flood damage, which standard home policies exclude. Many flood policies come through the National Flood Insurance Program, where a new policy has a 30-day wait, with a few exceptions such as a loan closing.
Inland flood coverage →
FS-1 NC DMV & law
North Carolina's electronic certificate of liability insurance. Your insurer sends it straight to NCDMV to show a vehicle has coverage; you never carry or print it.
What is an FS-1? →
Full coverage Auto
An everyday name, not a legal term, for an auto policy with liability plus comprehensive and collision. Even full coverage has limits and deductibles, so check what yours actually includes.

G

Gap insurance Auto
Pays the difference between what your car is worth and what you still owe on the loan or lease if the car is totaled or stolen. It matters most in the first years of a loan.
When the payout does not cover the loan →

H

Highway use tax NC DMV & law
North Carolina's 3% tax charged when a vehicle title is transferred, figured at a dealer on the price plus the doc fee, minus your trade-in (N.C.G.S. 105-187.3). It is no more than $250 if the car has been titled in your name in another state for at least 90 days (105-187.6).
NC car cost calculator →

I

Independent agent Buying & billing
An agent appointed with several insurance companies who can compare them for you, instead of selling only one company's policies.
Independent vs. captive agents →

L

Lapse in coverage NC DMV & law
Any gap when a registered vehicle has no liability insurance. NCDMV sends a notice, and as of October 1, 2026 you must respond by the date printed on it (30 days). A lapse can bring a penalty and can raise what you pay for insurance.
Car insurance after a lapse →
Liability-only Auto
An auto policy with the required liability, uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage but without comprehensive or collision, so it does not pay to fix your own car. Common on older, paid-off vehicles.
Liability-only car insurance in NC →

M

Medical payments (MedPay) Auto
Optional auto coverage that pays medical bills for you and your passengers after a crash, no matter who is at fault. It is not required in North Carolina.
Is MedPay required in NC? →

N

NC Rate Bureau NC DMV & law
The organization that files base rates for auto and home insurance in North Carolina with the Commissioner of Insurance. Companies can file lower rates or use consent to rate, so prices still differ from one company to the next (N.C.G.S. 58-36-30).
NCJUA (NC FAIR Plan) Home
The North Carolina Joint Underwriting Association, the state's FAIR Plan. It offers basic property coverage for homes that regular companies will not insure, and it is meant to be a last resort. It does not cover the coastal Beach Area, which has its own plan.
The NC FAIR Plan →

P

Personal umbrella policy Buying & billing
Extra liability coverage, often $1 million, that pays after your auto or home liability limits are used up. It protects savings and future wages from a large lawsuit.
Umbrella calculator →
Prayer for judgment continued (PJC) NC DMV & law
A North Carolina court option where a judge does not enter judgment after a guilty or no-contest plea on a traffic ticket. A PJC for a moving violation adds no insurance points unless someone in the household already has another one: in the past five years if that one was granted on or after July 1, 2025, or the past three years if it was granted before then.
SDIP points calculator →
Premium Buying & billing
The price you pay for an insurance policy, billed in full or in installments. A quote shows a premium; coverage does not start until a policy is bound.
Property damage liability Auto
Pays for damage you cause to other people's property, usually their car, but also fences, mailboxes and buildings. North Carolina requires at least $50,000.

R

Rental reimbursement Auto
Optional auto coverage that pays for a rental car, up to a daily and total limit, while your car is being repaired after a covered claim.
Rental reimbursement explained →
Renters insurance (HO-4) Home
Covers a tenant's belongings, liability and extra living costs. The landlord's policy covers the building, not what you own.
Renters insurance in NC →
Replacement cost Buying & billing
Pays what it costs to repair or replace damaged property with new property of like kind and quality, without taking off for age or wear (compare actual cash value).

S

SDIP (insurance points) NC DMV & law
North Carolina's Safe Driver Incentive Plan. Insurers add insurance points for at-fault accidents and moving violations, which raise your auto premium, usually for three policy years. Convictions worth four or more points, other than speeding, count for five years if they happened on or after July 1, 2025. SDIP points are separate from NCDMV license points.
SDIP points calculator →

T

Tag & Tax Together NC DMV & law
North Carolina's system for paying your yearly vehicle property tax at the same time as your registration renewal. You cannot renew without paying the tax.
NC car cost calculator →
Total loss Auto
When a car is not worth repairing. In North Carolina, when repairs would cost 75% or more of the car's actual cash value before the crash, the insurer must treat it as a total loss and owes that value.
The 75% total loss rule →

U

Underinsured motorist (UIM) Auto
Pays for your injuries when the at-fault driver has insurance but not enough to cover your losses. North Carolina law requires it on personal auto liability policies, at the same limits as your uninsured motorist coverage (N.C.G.S. 20-279.21).
UM and UIM explained →
Uninsured motorist (UM) Auto
Pays for your injuries and, with property damage UM, your car when the at-fault driver has no insurance. North Carolina auto policies include it, and it also applies to many hit-and-run crashes.
Uninsured motorist guide →

W

Water backup coverage Home
An add-on to a home policy for water that backs up through drains or sewers or overflows from a sump pump. A standard home policy does not cover it, and it is not flood insurance.
Do you need water backup coverage? →
Wind and hail deductible Home
A separate home deductible for wind and hail damage, often a percentage of your Coverage A amount instead of a flat dollar figure. A 2% deductible with $300,000 of Coverage A is $6,000.
How NC wind and hail deductibles work →
