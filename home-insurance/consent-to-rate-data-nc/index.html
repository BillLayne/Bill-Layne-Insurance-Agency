Consent to Rate in North Carolina: how many home policies carry the surcharge, by territory (2022–2025)

Every year the Commissioner of Insurance publishes how many North Carolina homeowners policies were written above the approved rate, and by how much, for each of the state’s 29 rating territories. We turned the four published summaries into one table, a county lookup and a download.

Source: NC Department of Insurance, Aggregate Consent-to-Rate Data Summaries under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-30(b2). Owners forms HO2, HO3, HO5, HO7 and HO8 written under Rate Bureau jurisdiction. These are statewide statistics, not quotes.

Quick answer: In 2025, 62.9% of the 2,414,544 North Carolina homeowners policies reported to the Department of Insurance were written with a consent-to-rate premium above the approved manual rate, up from 40.3% in 2022. On those policies the consented premium averaged $724 more than the manual premium (48.1% above it). The share ranges from 30.8% in territory 130 to 72.6% in territory 170; Surry County’s territory 310 sits at 68.7%.

Statewide: the surcharge went from a minority to a majority of policies

In 2022, four in ten Rate Bureau homeowners policies carried a consent-to-rate premium. By 2025 it was more than six in ten, and the average amount above the approved premium rose every year. Consent to rate stopped being the exception for hard-to-place homes and became how most North Carolina home policies are priced.

What the numbers mean for your renewal

A consent-to-rate premium is not a penalty for something you did. It is a company’s decision to charge more than the rate approved for your territory, disclosed on the policy as the law requires. When most policies in a territory carry one, the “approved rate” has become a floor that few companies actually use.

Two things follow. First, a surcharge on your renewal is normal and not a mark against you. Second, because the surcharge is each company’s choice, the same house can be priced at the approved rate by one company and well above it by another, so the renewal is worth shopping. Our consent-to-rate guide explains the notice line by line, and in our agency’s experience NC Grange Mutual does not use consent-to-rate pricing on North Carolina homes.

Definitions used on this page

Manual premium
The premium at the rate approved for the territory and policy form, with the normal factors and endorsements applied. Also called the approved rate.
Consented premium
What the company actually charged on a consent-to-rate policy: the manual premium plus the amount above it.
Share with CTR
Consent-to-rate policies written divided by all policies written in the territory that year.
Avg. difference per CTR policy
(Consented premium minus manual premium) divided by the number of consent-to-rate policies.
Markup over manual
The same difference as a percentage of the manual premium. Our calculation from NCDOI’s totals.
Territory
One of 29 NC Rate Bureau homeowners rating territories (110–390), assigned by county, or by ZIP code in five coastal counties.

Method and sources

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-30(b2) requires every Rate Bureau member writing homeowners insurance to report, for each territory, the policies written, the premium written, the number of consent-to-rate policies and their manual and consented premiums. The Commissioner publishes only the aggregate across all insurers. We transcribed the four published summaries, recomputed every percentage and average from the reported totals as a check (two territory-year percentages differ from NCDOI’s rounding by 0.1 point; we show NCDOI’s figures), and added the markup percentage, which NCDOI does not publish. County-to-territory assignments follow the NC Rate Bureau’s revised homeowners territory definitions effective June 1, 2015.

Questions about consent to rate in North Carolina

What is a consent-to-rate surcharge in North Carolina?
It is the amount a home insurer charges above the manual (approved) premium filed through the North Carolina Rate Bureau. N.C. Gen. Stat. 58-36-30(b) allows it as long as the policy carries the required disclosure; since January 1, 2019 paying the premium counts as consent. The surcharge shows up on the renewal as the difference between the two premiums.
What share of North Carolina home policies have a consent-to-rate surcharge?
In 2025, 62.9% of the 2,414,544 homeowners policies written under Rate Bureau jurisdiction carried a consent-to-rate surcharge, according to the NC Department of Insurance aggregate data call. That share was 40.3% in 2022.
How much is the average consent-to-rate surcharge in NC?
Statewide, the average difference between the approved premium and the consented premium was $724 per consent-to-rate policy in 2025, up from $364 in 2022. On those policies the consented premium ran 48.1% above the manual premium. Territories vary widely; use the county lookup on this page.
Which NC territory has the highest consent-to-rate rate?
In 2025, territory 170 (Gates, Hertford counties) had the highest share at 72.6% of policies. The lowest was territory 130 (Currituck, Dare, Hyde, Pamlico) at 30.8%.
What is a homeowners rating territory and how do I find mine?
The North Carolina Rate Bureau divides the state into 29 homeowners rating territories (codes 110 through 390), effective June 1, 2015. Most counties belong to one territory; Brunswick, Carteret, New Hanover, Onslow and Pender are split by ZIP code, and the Outer Banks beach areas of Currituck, Dare and Hyde are territory 110. Pick your county in the lookup on this page to see your territory and its numbers.
Where does this data come from?
From the NC Department of Insurance's annual Aggregate Consent-to-Rate Data Summaries, which the Commissioner must publish under N.C. Gen. Stat. 58-36-30(b2). Insurers report owners-form policies (HO2, HO3, HO5, HO7, HO8) by territory; NCDOI publishes only the aggregate. Bill Layne Insurance transcribed the four published summaries (2022 through 2025) and recomputed the percentages as a check. Nothing here is a quote or a rate.
Can I avoid a consent-to-rate surcharge?
Sometimes. The surcharge is a company decision about your home, so another company may price the same house at or near the approved rate. In our agency's experience NC Grange Mutual does not use consent-to-rate pricing on North Carolina homeowners policies. Shopping the renewal is the only way to know; a surcharge is not a mark against you.
