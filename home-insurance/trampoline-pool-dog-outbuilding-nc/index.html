Nonrenewed Over a Trampoline, Pool, Dog or Outbuilding? There Is Another Way to Insure the House

The house is solid. The roof is fine. Then a letter says the policy will not be renewed because of a trampoline in the backyard, the pool, the dog on the porch or a shed by the tree line. One item on a checklist, and a good home gets shown the door. NC Grange Mutual looks at the whole house, and in many cases a signed exclusion for the one item keeps the rest of the policy in force. Quick answer:

Often, yes, the house can stay insured. Most large home insurers screen every application against automatic rules, so a trampoline, a swimming pool, a dog on a breed list or an outbuilding in poor repair can end a policy before anyone looks at the property. NC Grange Mutual underwrites each North Carolina home individually, and in our agency’s experience it has signed exclusion forms for exactly these four items, so you can often keep the item and keep the policy. Do this the week the letter arrives

North Carolina gives you 45 days’ written notice before a nonrenewal, and the notice must state the precise reason. That window is generous only if you use the front of it.

Find the precise reason

The letter has to say why. That one sentence tells us which carriers are worth approaching for your house and which are not.

Write down the expiration date

The policy expiration date, not the letter date, is the day coverage has to be in place by. Put it on the calendar.

Photograph the item they objected to

The trampoline and its net, the pool fence and gate, the dog, the outbuilding from several angles. A real photo beats an aerial image every time.

Gather proof of repairs

Receipts, a new roof invoice on the shed, a replaced gate latch, an anchored trampoline enclosure. A corrected condition is usually underwritable; an undocumented one usually is not.

Ask about a signed exclusion

Ask directly whether a carrier will keep the policy in force with a signed exclusion for the one item. It is not available everywhere, but it is the option most homeowners never hear about.

Get the new policy bound before the old one ends

Do not let the old policy lapse first. A gap in coverage can affect your mortgage and your eligibility at the next carrier.

Bill’s two cents: nine out of ten people call me in week six. The trampoline has not moved and the dog has not changed, but now we are rushing, and rushing is how you end up paying more for less coverage than you had. The letter is a deadline, not a verdict.

Four items that end a policy, and what we do instead

Each of these is a company rule, not a judgment about your home. Different company, different rule.

Trampoline

A liability risk, not a property risk

Nobody is worried a trampoline will burn the house down. It is filed with pools and diving boards as an attractive nuisance: children get hurt on them, and the injuries can be serious enough to reach the liability limit. Because it is easy to spot, many national carriers apply one rule everywhere: no trampoline, no policy. That rule does not know whether yours has a full enclosure, is anchored, or sits a quarter mile from the nearest neighbor.

What we do: NC Grange can often write the home with a signed trampoline exclusion. You keep the trampoline; the rest of the policy stays in force.

Swimming pool

The same category, with more questions

A pool is the classic attractive nuisance. Carriers that will cover one usually want a fence with a self-closing, self-latching gate, often no diving board or slide, and a removable ladder on an above-ground pool. An in-ground pool also adds to the value of your other structures. Miss one condition and some companies decline the whole house rather than ask.

What we do: NC Grange has a signed pool exclusion form, so the home can be insured while the pool itself is excluded from liability coverage. If you would rather have the pool covered, say so: that is a different quote, and the fence, gate and diving-board details decide it.

Dog

Breed lists decide before a person reads the file

Many carriers keep an internal breed list, and an application that names a dog on it is declined before anyone reads the rest. Some companies have moved to bite history instead, which is fairer, but it is not universal. A family that has had the same gentle dog for nine years gets the same answer as a stranger with a bite on record.

What we do: NC Grange underwrites the household, not the breed list, and has a signed dog exclusion form that removes liability coverage for that specific dog while everything else stays in force. A dog with a documented bite is a harder conversation at any carrier, and we will tell you so.

Outbuilding

The aerial photo problem

A working property in Surry, Yadkin or Wilkes County might have a hay barn, a pole shed, a smokehouse and a tobacco building that has not held tobacco since the eighties. None of them threaten the house. All of them show up from the air. Most carriers now order aerial imagery at renewal, software flags a sagging roofline or a tarp, and the file lands in front of an underwriter who has never driven past the place.

What we do: NC Grange can often exclude the specific outbuilding with a signed form and insure the house and the rest of the property. You keep the barn; the house stays covered. When a building truly is past saving, sometimes the cheapest fix is a dumpster, and we will say that too.

What a signed exclusion really does

It is not a loophole and it is not free. Understand the trade before you sign it.

A signed exclusion is an endorsement you sign stating that the policy will not respond to a claim arising from one specific item: that trampoline, that pool, that dog, or that named outbuilding. The dwelling, the contents, the other structures and the rest of your personal liability all stay in force. What you give up is coverage for one exposure, and you accept that exposure personally.

What you keepThe home, contents, other structures, and liability for everything else on the policy.
What you give upLiability and medical payments arising from the excluded item, or coverage for the excluded structure itself.
What it costs youA signature, and a real understanding of what happens if that dog ever bites or a visitor is hurt on the trampoline.

In our agency’s experience, NC Grange Mutual has separate signed exclusion forms for a trampoline, a swimming pool, a dog and an outbuilding. Each one names the item and nothing else. NC Grange makes the final underwriting decision on every property, and no carrier accepts everything.

What I tell my clients: I will not put an exclusion in front of you without explaining exactly what it turns off. Some folks sign it in ten seconds. Some decide the dog goes to the fenced side of the yard and we look for a carrier that will cover him. Both are fine answers. They are just yours to make, not mine.

What actually moves an underwriter

Aerial imagery starts the conversation. Ground-level photos and receipts are how you finish it. Detached structures generally fall under Coverage B on a homeowners policy, so their condition is legitimately part of the picture, but a photo taken from 3,000 feet cannot tell a decommissioned barn from a hazard.

  • Ground-level photos from three or four angles, dated, showing the structure or the item as it actually stands.
  • Repair documentation: a new metal roof on the shed, replaced doors, a new gate latch, cleared debris.
  • An honest description of use: stored equipment, hay, nothing at all. Vacant and unmaintained reads worse than in-use.
  • For a pool: fence height, the gate latch, whether there is a diving board or slide, and whether the pool is in use.
  • For a dog: breed, age, how long you have had it, and a plain statement about bite history.

This is where a carrier that writes farm business has a real advantage. A company that insures machinery, livestock and outbuildings every day reads a pole barn differently than one whose book is suburban subdivisions.

The honest limits

NC Grange does not insure every home, and we cannot promise acceptance or a particular price. A roof that is failing, a house that is vacant, unrepaired damage, a dog with a bite on record or a run of recent claims will be a problem with every carrier, including NC Grange. What we can promise is that the decision will be made by a person looking at your property, and that we will shop your home with every carrier we represent, not just one.

We are an independent agency. If another company will cover the trampoline or the dog outright and that fits you better, that is the quote we will recommend.

Questions homeowners ask us

Can I keep my trampoline and still get homeowners insurance in North Carolina?
Often, yes. Some North Carolina carriers will not write a home with a trampoline at all, but others will consider it, and some will offer a signed liability exclusion so the trampoline is not covered while the rest of the policy stays in force. In our agency's experience, NC Grange Mutual has a signed trampoline exclusion form and underwrites each home individually. Whether that option is available depends on the carrier and the individual risk.
Does a swimming pool make my home harder to insure in NC?
A pool is a liability exposure, so many carriers either decline it, add conditions such as a fence with a self-latching gate and no diving board, or price it higher. In our agency's experience, NC Grange Mutual has a signed pool exclusion form, which lets the home be insured while the pool itself is excluded from liability coverage. If you want the pool covered rather than excluded, that is a different quote and the fence, gate and diving-board details matter.
Does NC Grange Mutual insure homes with dogs?
NC Grange Mutual underwrites each property and each household individually rather than applying one blanket breed rule to every application. A dog with no bite history is often workable, and in our agency's experience NC Grange has a signed dog exclusion form that removes liability coverage for that specific dog while the rest of the policy stays in force. A dog with a documented bite is a harder conversation at any carrier.
Why did my insurance company nonrenew me over an old shed or barn?
Many carriers inspect homes with aerial imagery now, and an outbuilding with a failing roof, leaning walls or debris around it shows up clearly. Detached structures are usually covered under Coverage B, so their condition is part of the underwriting picture even though nobody lives in them. In our agency's experience, NC Grange Mutual can often exclude the specific outbuilding with a signed form and insure the house and the rest of the property.
What is a signed exclusion on a homeowners policy, and what do I give up?
It is an endorsement you sign that removes coverage for one specific item, such as a trampoline, a swimming pool, a particular dog or a named outbuilding. You keep the home, the contents and the rest of your liability coverage. If someone is injured by the excluded item, or the excluded structure is damaged, the policy will not respond, so you are accepting that risk personally. Understand the trade before you sign it.
How much notice does a North Carolina insurer have to give before nonrenewal?
Under N.C. Gen. Stat. 58-41-20, an insurer must mail or deliver written notice of nonrenewal at least 45 days before the policy expires, and the notice must state the precise reason. A mid-term cancellation for most permitted reasons requires at least 15 days' notice under N.C. Gen. Stat. 58-41-15.
What if the nonrenewal is about claims, not the backyard?
Then the fix is different. A trampoline, pool, dog or outbuilding can usually be handled with a signed exclusion; a run of claims cannot. How many claims it takes before a company nonrenews depends on the carrier, the type of claim and the dollar amount, and some carriers count claims against the house as well as the owner. Read our guide to home insurance cancelled or nonrenewed after claims in North Carolina, then send us the letter and we will tell you which carriers are realistic.
I already got the nonrenewal letter. Is it too late to shop?
No. The 45-day notice period exists so you have time to place coverage elsewhere. Start the day the letter arrives rather than the week the policy expires, because photos, repair documentation and underwriting review all take time, and a gap in coverage can affect both your mortgage and your eligibility at the next carrier.

It is one of the larger writers of farm and rural property coverage in the state and is sold only through independent agents.
