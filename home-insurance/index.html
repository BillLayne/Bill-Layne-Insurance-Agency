Understand what protects your home.

Four important parts of your coverage.

Dwelling

Covers the physical structure of your home (walls, roof, foundation).

Belongings

Protects furniture, electronics, and clothes, even when traveling.

Liability

Financial protection if someone gets injured on your property.

Loss of Use

Pays for hotels and food if a covered claim forces you to move out.

Coverage is subject to your policy’s terms, limits, deductibles and exclusions.

Focus on the details that matter.

Rebuild cost is not market value

Dwelling coverage should focus on what it may cost to rebuild the home, not what the tax card or real estate market says it is worth.

Use dwelling calculator

Wind, hail, roof age and deductibles

North Carolina homeowners should review roof age, cosmetic damage terms, wind/hail deductibles, and whether older roofs are settled differently.

Example: A lower premium can hide a higher wind/hail deductible or a roof settlement limitation.

Water damage is not all the same

A burst pipe, sump backup, seepage, and outside flood can be treated very differently. Some need endorsements or a separate flood policy.

Example: Rising water from outside is usually flood, not standard homeowners insurance.

Policy form changes the answer

An HO-3, HO-5, HO-6, HO-7, DP-2, DP-3, renters policy, and flood policy do not protect the same things.

A few common questions

What does standard home insurance NOT cover?
Standard policies exclude floods, earthquakes, maintenance issues, and pest infestations. Flood insurance requires a separate policy.
How much dwelling coverage do I need?
You need enough to REBUILD your home at current labor and material costs, which is often different from your home's market value.
What is Replacement Cost vs ACV?
Replacement Cost pays for a new item. Actual Cash Value (ACV) pays for the item minus depreciation (what it's worth used). We recommend Replacement Cost.
Does home insurance cover flood damage in NC?
No. Standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage. You need a separate flood insurance policy, typically through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) or a private flood insurer. Even if you're not in a designated flood zone, over 25% of flood claims come from moderate-to-low risk areas. We can help you get a flood quote.
How does my roof age affect my home insurance rate?
Roof age is one of the biggest rating factors. A newer roof (under 10 years) can qualify for significant discounts. Roofs over 20 years may face higher premiums or limited coverage. Some carriers switch from replacement cost to actual cash value (ACV) for older roofs. If you've recently replaced your roof, make sure your agent knows — it could lower your premium.
What is a wind/hail deductible and do I need one in NC?
Many NC homeowners policies have a separate wind/hail deductible, often 1-2% of your dwelling coverage. This means if your home is insured for $300,000, your wind/hail deductible could be $3,000-$6,000 — much higher than your standard deductible. Given NC's exposure to hurricanes and severe storms, understanding this deductible is critical.
Should I bundle my home and auto insurance?
In most cases, yes. Bundling home and auto with the same carrier usually earns a multi-policy discount. However, as an independent agency, we sometimes find that splitting policies between two carriers actually saves more. We'll compare both options and show you how the two approaches compare.
What happens if a tree falls on my house?
Good news — a tree falling on your home is typically covered under standard homeowners insurance, whether it's your tree or a neighbor's. Your policy covers the structural damage and usually includes some coverage for debris removal. However, a tree that falls in your yard but doesn't hit anything is generally not covered. Preventive tree removal is also not covered.
Is homeowners insurance required by law in North Carolina?
North Carolina law does not require homeowners insurance by itself. If you have a mortgage, your lender will require it. Even if your home is paid off, coverage is still strongly recommended because one fire, storm, liability claim, or major water loss could put the full value of the home at risk.
Why is my NC homeowners insurance so expensive?
Premiums have risen because construction costs, severe weather losses, roof claims, and reinsurance costs have all increased. The best move is to compare multiple carriers, review your deductible, confirm roof and renovation updates, and check whether bundling or carrier-specific discounts truly help your situation.
Why compare 9+ home insurance carriers in North Carolina?
Home insurance companies view roof age, claims history, construction type, fire protection, occupancy, location, and weather risk differently. Comparing 9+ home insurance carriers and specialty markets gives our team more ways to match your home to the right coverage path.
Can a DP-2 policy be used for a primary home in North Carolina?
Yes, in some cases. DP policies are often used for rental, secondary, older, vacant, or hard-to-place homes, but in North Carolina some DP policies can also be used for primary homes through markets such as NCJUA. The right answer depends on occupancy, eligibility, underwriting, and endorsements.
What is the difference between HO-3 and DP-2 in NC?
An HO-3 is a standard homeowners package for many owner-occupied homes. A DP-2 is a broad named-peril dwelling policy, which means the cause of loss generally must be listed in the policy. DP-2 may not automatically include the same personal property, liability, or loss-of-use features as an HO-3.
Why should I create a home inventory before a claim?
A home inventory helps document what you owned before a fire, theft, tornado, or water loss. Room photos, serial numbers, receipts, and item lists can make the claim process easier and reduce the chance of forgetting important belongings.
