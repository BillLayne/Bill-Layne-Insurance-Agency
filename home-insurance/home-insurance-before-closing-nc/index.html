Home Insurance Before Closing: What Your Lender Needs

Your lender will want proof of home insurance before you close. Here is what to send, when to start, and what to do if the closing date moves.

Quick Answer

North Carolina law does not require home insurance, but if you are borrowing to buy, your lender will generally require it before closing. Start your quote as soon as you are under contract. Get the lender’s mortgagee clause and loan number, set the policy to start on your closing date, and make sure the lender and your closing attorney have proof of insurance before closing day. A quote alone is not proof of coverage.

Your closing checklist, step by step

Most delays we see come from starting late. Here is the order that keeps closing day calm.

  1. 1
    When your offer is accepted

    Send us the address early

    Do this during your due diligence period, while you can still negotiate or walk away. We check which companies will write the home before you are committed.

    • Ask the seller for the home’s claims history (a CLUE report). Why it matters
    • Share your home inspection if you have it, especially anything about the roof, wiring, plumbing or heating.
  2. 2
    Before due diligence ends

    Get a written quote you can actually buy

    Compare coverage, not just price: the dwelling amount, the deductible, and whether a roof claim is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value. We compare eight North Carolina home insurance companies for you.

  3. 3
    As soon as you have a lender

    Get the lender’s instructions

    • Mortgagee clause: the lender’s exact name and address as it should appear on the policy.
    • Loan number, once it is assigned.
    • Coverage they require, such as a minimum dwelling amount or a highest allowed deductible.
    • Who gets proof: usually the lender and your closing attorney.
    • How the first year is paid: before closing, at the closing table, or through escrow. Ask the lender.
  4. 4
    One to two weeks before closing

    Bind the policy for your closing date

    The policy should start the day the home becomes yours. We set the effective date, list your lender, and send proof of insurance to the lender and the closing attorney.

  5. 5
    If closing moves

    Call or text us the new date

    A closing that moves is common. Tell us right away so we can change the start date and send updated proof. Don’t let the policy start after you take ownership.

A quote is not proof of insurance

A quote is a price. Your lender needs a policy that has been bound, with the lender listed and a start date. Asking for a quote, here or anywhere, does not start coverage until a licensed agent confirms it.

Flood insurance and closing

Most home policies do not cover flood. If the home is in a special flood hazard area and your loan comes from a federally regulated lender or a federal agency, federal law requires flood insurance on the building for the life of the loan (42 U.S.C. 4012a).

A new National Flood Insurance Program policy usually has a 30-day wait. When you buy it as part of the loan, it can start at closing if you apply and pay at or before closing (44 CFR 61.11). Your lender will tell you if the home is in a flood zone, so ask early.

How much coverage on the house

The dwelling amount should be what it would cost to rebuild the house, not the price you paid. The land is not part of it.

The NC Department of Insurance says to carry at least 80% of the full replacement cost. If you carry less, the company may not pay the full cost to rebuild. Your lender may also set a minimum.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a home insurance quote proof of coverage for closing?

No. A quote is a price. Your lender needs proof of a policy that has been bound, with the lender listed as mortgagee and a start date on or before closing. We send that proof to your lender and closing attorney once you choose a policy.

Is home insurance required by law in North Carolina?

No. The NC Department of Insurance says home insurance is not required by North Carolina law, but a lender may require it if the home is mortgaged. If you are borrowing to buy, plan on needing it before closing. More on what the law requires.

When should I start shopping for home insurance when buying a house?

As soon as your offer is accepted. Starting during your due diligence period gives you time to find out whether the home can be insured, and at what price, while you can still negotiate or walk away.

What happens if my closing date changes?

Call or text us the new date. We change the policy’s start date and send updated proof to your lender and closing attorney. The policy should start the day the home becomes yours, not later.

Do I need flood insurance to close on a house in North Carolina?

Only in some cases. If the home is in a special flood hazard area and the loan is from a federally regulated lender or a federal agency, federal law requires flood insurance (42 U.S.C. 4012a). A flood policy bought as part of the loan can start at closing if you apply and pay at or before closing (44 CFR 61.11).

Who pays the first year of home insurance when I buy a house?

You do. Depending on your lender, the first year may be paid before closing or at the closing table, and later years are often paid from an escrow account with your mortgage payment. Ask your lender how they want it handled.
