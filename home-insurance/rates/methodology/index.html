NC Home Insurance Rate Index: Methodology

Every premium example on this site is a real quote for one standardized, hypothetical home, run through the agency's carrier rating systems on a stated date. This page is the fine print behind those numbers.

Why we publish rate examples at all

National websites that publish an "average North Carolina home insurance cost" use modeled data from the same vendor and still disagree with each other by thousands of dollars a year. None of those figures is a quote for a real house in Elkin, Jonesville, or Mount Airy. Our index instead shows what a specific, fully described example home was quoted for in a specific town on a specific date, with every assumption disclosed, so a homeowner can see how the town, the fire protection class, and the rating territory move the number.

How each example is quoted

  1. The example home is entered into the rating system of every homeowners carrier the agency represents that writes in the town. The current list is on our carriers page.
  2. The published figure is the lowest annual premium returned among those carriers for the standardized assumptions. We publish it as "lowest quoted among the carriers we represent," not as a market-wide minimum.
  3. The quote PDF and quote number for every published example are kept on file at the agency so any figure can be traced back to its source.
  4. The quote date shown on the page is the day the rater produced the number. The last-verified date is the most recent day we re-ran or re-checked it.

What we publish and what we withhold

Disclosure rules for every rate page
PublishedThe example annual premium, town, county, ZIP, rating territory, protection class, coverage assumptions, quote date, last-verified date, rate basis, quarter-over-quarter change, and the June 1 North Carolina Rate Bureau base-rate context
WithheldAnything tied to a real customer; the street-level location of the example property; per-carrier premiums by name unless that carrier has approved it in writing; claims-history assumptions beyond "no claims in five years"; any wording such as "cheapest," "guaranteed," "your rate," or "save $X"

Refresh cadence and version history

  • Quarterly (February, May, August, November): every town's example is re-run. The figure, quoted date, modified date, and change log are updated together.
  • Within two weeks of June 1 each year: mandatory refresh, because the North Carolina Rate Bureau homeowners base-rate change takes effect June 1.
  • On any carrier filing that affects the agency's book: affected towns are spot-refreshed.
  • Examples are updated in place at the same URL. Previous figures are kept in a collapsed "previous examples" section and in the CSV download so year-over-year change can be cited. Old figures are never shown as current.
  • A town that has not had a fresh quote in twelve months is removed from the index rather than left stale.

Compliance framing

N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-63-15 prohibits misrepresentation and false advertising of policy terms, and carrier agency agreements generally require marketing approval before a carrier's name appears next to a premium. Every rate page therefore states that the figure is one dated example for a hypothetical standardized home, not an offer, average, or guarantee; that eligibility and premium are determined by the carrier; and whether the example is an approved rate or a consent-to-rate premium.
