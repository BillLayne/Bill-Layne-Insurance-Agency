Four situations where the usual companies say no — and what we do instead

Most home insurers in North Carolina run every application through a pricing model that starts with your credit and a list of automatic declines. NC Grange starts with the house. Here is what that means in each case.

Consent to rate

You got a consent-to-rate letter

The letter asks you to agree to a premium above the rate the state approved, or the company will not keep you. You do not have to sign it. It is a signal to shop.

What we do: NC Grange does not ask our clients to sign consent-to-rate forms, so it is one of the first quotes we run. Read our consent-to-rate guide to understand the letter first.

Non-renewal

You were non-renewed or dropped

A claim, a roof past a cut-off age, a trampoline, a dog breed — the reasons are often a box on a checklist rather than a judgement about your home.

What we do: NC Grange has a person look at the whole house. Here is how that played out for a client dropped over a trampoline.

Outbuildings

A shed or barn is the problem

Older properties around here come with outbuildings. When one is in poor repair, many companies will not write the house until it is fixed or torn down.

What we do: In many cases NC Grange can exclude the specific outbuilding and insure the house and the rest of the property. You keep the barn; the house stays covered.

Credit score

You are being priced on your credit

A divorce, a medical bill, a business setback or simply a thin file can push your home premium up for years — even though none of it changes the risk to the house.

What we do: NC Grange does not use a credit-based insurance score in rating. Your price reflects the property. More on how credit affects NC home rates.

Questions homeowners ask us

Is there a home insurance company in North Carolina that does not use my credit score?
Yes. In our agency's experience, NC Grange Mutual does not use a credit-based insurance score to rate its North Carolina homeowners policies. It rates the house itself: age, roof, updates, condition and claims history. Bill Layne Insurance in Elkin is an appointed NC Grange agency and can quote it for you.
What is a consent-to-rate letter and do I have to sign it?
A consent-to-rate form asks you to agree to a premium above the state-approved rate so the company will keep insuring your home. You are not required to sign it. It is a signal to shop. NC Grange Mutual does not ask our clients to sign consent-to-rate forms, so it is one of the first carriers we quote when a homeowner receives one.
My insurer wants me to tear down or repair an old shed or barn. Are there other options?
Often, yes. In many cases NC Grange Mutual can exclude a specific outbuilding that is in poor repair and insure the house and the rest of the property. That means you are not forced to demolish a barn or shed you rarely use just to keep coverage on your home. NC Grange makes the final decision after reviewing the property.
Does no credit score mean anyone can get a policy?
No. The house still has to be in reasonable shape. NC Grange looks at roof age and condition, wiring, plumbing and heating updates, whether the home is occupied, prior claims, and fire protection. Credit is simply not part of the price. Eligibility is decided by NC Grange after underwriting.
Does Bill Layne Insurance only quote NC Grange?
No. We are an independent agency and quote several North Carolina home insurance companies. NC Grange is the one we reach for when credit, a consent-to-rate letter, a non-renewal or an outbuilding is the problem, because it rates differently. If another carrier fits you better, we will tell you.
What do you need from me to quote my home?
Your address, the year the home was built, the year of the roof, any wiring, plumbing or heating updates, your current declarations page if you have one, and photos of any outbuildings. If you received a consent-to-rate or non-renewal letter, send that too. Most quotes are turned around the same or next business day.
