Who needs a boater education card

North Carolina requires anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 to complete a boating safety course approved by the National Association of State Boating Law Administrators before operating a vessel with a motor of 10 horsepower or more on public waters (G.S. 75A-16.2). The certificate is good for life and has to be aboard while you operate.

Operators born before 1988 are exempt, but many take the course anyway: it is a few hours online, and several of the carriers we represent apply a safety-course discount when the operator holds the card. Tell us if you have one when you ask for a quote.

Why it matters for insurance: operator experience and education are rating factors on every boat policy we write. A young operator with a card quotes differently from one without.

Registration and titling

The NC Wildlife Resources Commission registers vessels for one or three years. You must register:

  • Every motorized vessel used on public waters, including jet skis and other personal watercraft.
  • Sailboats longer than 14 feet at the load waterline.
  • Boats registered in another state that stay in North Carolina more than 60 consecutive days, and Coast Guard documented vessels operating here more than 60 consecutive days.

Exempt: canoes, kayaks, rowboats and rafts moved only by paddle, oar or current; boats used only on a private pond; and vessels kept unused on dry land.

Titling is required for motorized vessels and sailboats 14 feet or longer bought or transferred on or after January 1, 2007, and for every personal watercraft regardless of length. Lenders generally require a title before they finance a boat, which is also why a financed boat almost always needs full physical-damage coverage with the lender listed on the policy.

Renewals run through the Go Outdoors North Carolina vessel system, by phone, or at a wildlife service agent. Carry the registration certificate aboard and display the validation decals.

Life jackets

  • A wearable, properly sized life jacket must be aboard for every person on the boat.
  • Children under 13 must wear one whenever a recreational vessel is underway.
  • Everyone on a personal watercraft, and anyone being towed by one, must wear one at all times.
  • Boats from 16 feet to under 26 feet also need a throwable device (Type IV) aboard.

Boats under 26 feet also need a Type B-I fire extinguisher where required, a horn, whistle or bell, navigation lights, and a working muffler. Backfire flame arrestors are required on inboard and stern-drive engines.

Personal watercraft (jet ski) rules

Personal watercraft have their own section of the law (G.S. 75A-13.3). In short:

  • No one under 14 may operate a PWC on North Carolina waters.
  • Riders who are 14 or 15 may operate only if an adult of at least 18 is physically aboard, or if they carry proof of age and an approved boating-safety certificate and show it to an officer on request.
  • Owners who knowingly let someone under 16 operate outside those rules are themselves in violation.
  • No PWC may operate between sunset and sunrise.
  • The engine cut-off lanyard must be attached to the operator, and everyone aboard or being towed wears a life jacket.

Carriers rate PWCs separately from boats for exactly these reasons: more operators per machine, younger operators, and more towing. Our jet ski and PWC insurance guide covers what that means for your policy.

Boating under the influence

Operating any vessel while underway with an alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more, or while impaired by any substance, is a Class 2 misdemeanor under G.S. 75A-10, with a minimum fine set by statute. The impairment rule also reaches water skis, surfboards and non-motorized vessels. Reckless or negligent operation that endangers life, limb or property is a separate Class 2 misdemeanor.

A boating-under-the-influence conviction follows you onto future boat applications the same way a DWI follows a driver, so it changes both eligibility and price for years.

When an accident must be reported

Report a boating accident on public waters to the Wildlife Resources Commission, 24 hours a day at 800-662-7137, when any of these happen:

  • A death, an injury that needs medical treatment beyond first aid, or a person left unconscious or disabled for 24 hours or more.
  • Property damage, including damage to the vessels, above the state's reporting threshold (currently two thousand dollars).
  • A person disappears from a vessel in a way that suggests death or injury.

Report it to us the same day. Boat claims move faster when the carrier gets the Wildlife report number early, and the state report also protects you if the other operator carries no insurance. Our boat insurance FAQ explains uninsured boater coverage.

No-wake zones and wake responsibility

A no-wake zone means idle speed, slow enough to leave no appreciable wake. North Carolina has no statute that automatically creates a no-wake zone around every dock or pier; local zones are established by the Wildlife Resources Commission on application from the county or town, and on Lake Norman and Lake Wylie through the lake marine commissions. Read the buoys on each lake you visit.

Even outside a zone, the operator is responsible for damage the wake causes to docks, moored boats and swimmers. That is a liability claim, and it is the most common one we see from pontoon and wake-boat owners.

How these rules show up on your policy

  • Operator age and education are rating factors. Listing a 15-year-old PWC operator without a safety card can make a policy unquotable with some carriers.
  • Registration and HIN are required to bind coverage; a boat with no registration cannot be insured on the water.
  • Navigation limits on the policy must match where you actually boat, from inland lakes to coastal waters.
  • Violations for BUI, reckless operation or an unreported accident can lead to a declined claim or a non-renewal.

When you ask for a quote, we check every one of these against your answers. It is five minutes that keeps the price you were quoted and the price you pay the same.

Boating law questions we hear most

Do I need a boating license in North Carolina?

North Carolina has no boating license. It has a boater education requirement: anyone born on or after January 1, 1988 must carry an approved boater education certificate to operate a vessel with a motor of 10 horsepower or more on public waters.

How old do you have to be to drive a jet ski in NC?

Fourteen at the youngest. Riders who are 14 or 15 must either have an adult of at least 18 aboard or carry proof of age and an approved boating-safety certificate. Sixteen and older follow the general boater education rule.

Does a kayak or canoe need to be registered?

No. Vessels moved only by paddle, oar or current are exempt from registration in North Carolina. Add a trolling motor and it becomes a motorized vessel that must be registered.

Is boat insurance required by North Carolina law?

No. The state does not require insurance for privately owned recreational boats. Lenders require it on financed boats, and many marinas, lake communities and storage yards require proof of liability coverage.

Do children have to wear life jackets on a boat in NC?

Yes. Anyone under 13 must wear a life jacket whenever a recreational vessel is underway, and everyone on a personal watercraft or being towed by one wears one at all times, regardless of age.

What is the legal limit for operating a boat in North Carolina?

An alcohol concentration of 0.08 or more, or impairment by any substance, while operating a vessel underway. It is a Class 2 misdemeanor and it affects future boat insurance eligibility.
