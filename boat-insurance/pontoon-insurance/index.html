NORTH CAROLINA · PONTOON INSURANCE

Pontoon boat insurance
for North Carolina lakes.

Pontoons are the most common boat on W. Kerr Scott, High Rock and Lake Norman, and the most common boat we insure. Here is what a pontoon policy actually covers, what moves the price, and what to have ready so the quote is right the first time.

What a pontoon policy covers

  • Hull, motor and attached equipment. Physical damage to the boat itself from collision, storm, fire, theft, sinking and similar causes, settled on either agreed value or actual cash value.
  • Liability. Injury or property damage you cause to others: a swimmer, a dock, another boat, or a wake that swamps a kayak.
  • Medical payments. Injuries to your guests regardless of fault.
  • Uninsured boater. Injuries caused by an operator who carries no liability insurance, which is common because North Carolina does not require boat insurance.
  • Trailer. Added to the policy by value so it is covered off the road as well as on it.
  • Personal effects, fishing gear and electronics. Separate limits that can usually be raised.
  • On-water towing and wreck removal. Optional on most policies and cheap relative to the cost of a tow across a big lake.

What drives the price of a pontoon policy

  • Length, horsepower and value. A 20-foot pontoon with a modest outboard is a different risk from a 25-foot tritoon with a large motor that can pull a wakeboarder.
  • Settlement basis. Agreed value costs more than actual cash value because it pays the full listed amount after a total loss. Our agreed value guide explains the trade.
  • Where it is kept. A pontoon stored in a dry-stack or on a trailer at home rates differently from one that stays in a slip all season.
  • Operators. Ages, boating experience, boater education cards and driving records of everyone who will run the boat.
  • Navigation area. Inland lakes only is the least expensive. Coastal use changes the policy.
  • Lay-up period. Months the boat is out of the water. Using the boat during a declared lay-up can void physical-damage coverage, so be honest about winter trips.
  • Deductibles. Hull and liability deductibles are set separately on most boat policies.

Tritoons, big motors and tow sports

Three-tube pontoons with large outboards changed how carriers look at the category. Once a pontoon can tow a tube or a wakeboard, the liability exposure looks more like a ski boat. Expect questions about maximum horsepower, whether you tow, and who drives while someone is behind the boat. North Carolina also requires an observer or a wide-angle mirror when towing, and everyone being towed wears a life jacket.

If you added a bigger motor after you bought the boat, tell us. An undisclosed repower is one of the few things that can leave a claim unpaid.

Slips, lake houses and trailers

Where the pontoon lives when you are not on it matters as much as where you run it:

  • Slip or lift at a marina. Many marinas require proof of liability coverage before assigning the slip. Ask for the certificate when we bind.
  • Lift at a lake house. Your homeowners policy on the lake house may provide limited coverage for small boats, but a pontoon generally needs its own policy. Our guide on whether homeowners insurance covers a boat explains where that line falls.
  • Trailered at home. While towing, your auto policy's liability generally extends to the trailer, but damage to the boat and trailer themselves comes from the boat policy. List the trailer.

What to have ready for a pontoon quote

  • Year, make, model, length and hull identification number.
  • Motor year, make and horsepower, and whether it was repowered.
  • Trailer year, make and value if you want it covered.
  • Every operator's name, date of birth, boating experience and boater education status.
  • Where it is stored, where you boat, and any months it is out of the water.
  • Lender name and address if financed.
  • Current declarations page if you are already insured. A photo is enough.

Pontoon insurance questions

How much is pontoon boat insurance in North Carolina?

It depends on the boat's value, length and horsepower, how the hull is valued, where it is stored, who operates it and the limits you choose. We quote each pontoon across four carriers rather than giving a generic figure, because the spread between carriers on the same boat is often the biggest saving available.

Does my homeowners policy cover my pontoon?

Usually only a little. Most North Carolina homeowners policies limit watercraft property coverage to a small amount and restrict liability to small boats and low-horsepower motors. A pontoon generally needs its own policy.

Is a tritoon more expensive to insure than a pontoon?

Often, because tritoons carry larger motors and are used for towing, which raises both the hull value and the liability exposure. The difference is usually modest when the operators are experienced.

Do I need insurance to keep my pontoon in a marina slip?

North Carolina does not require it, but most marinas and many lake communities require proof of liability coverage before assigning a slip. We can issue the certificate the day the policy binds.

Should I insure my pontoon for agreed value or actual cash value?

Newer and financed pontoons are usually written on agreed value so a total loss pays the amount listed on the policy with no depreciation. Older boats are often written on actual cash value to save premium. We show you both prices.
