Do I Need Insurance to Register a Moped in NC?

Mopeds get a plate in North Carolina, and a plate means insurance. Here's what the law requires and how to get covered before you register.

Quick answer

Yes. North Carolina requires liability insurance for a moped you register for road use under N.C.G.S. § 20-309(a). Before you apply for an NCDMV plate, confirm your scooter meets the state’s moped definition, carry at least 50/100/50 liability limits, and keep coverage active while registered.

Insurance first, then the plate.

Mopeds are a popular, low-cost way to get around, especially for students, commuters and anyone without a car. In North Carolina they're registered like small motorcycles, so they need liability insurance too. What does North Carolina law say about moped insurance?

The state's financial responsibility law spells it out, including mopeds by name:

"No motor vehicle shall be registered in this State unless the owner at the time of registration provides proof of financial responsibility for the operation of such motor vehicle, as provided in this Article. The owner of each motor vehicle registered in this State shall maintain financial responsibility continuously throughout the period of registration. For purposes of this Article, the term 'motor vehicle' includes mopeds, as that term is defined in G.S. 20-4.01."— N.C.G.S. § 20-309(a)

For policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, the minimum liability limits are 50/100/50: $50,000 per person and $100,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $50,000 for property damage (N.C.G.S. § 20-279.21; NCDOI motorcycle insurance guide). Some older NCDMV pages still show the previous lower limits, so go by the statute and the Department of Insurance.

Engine size and top speed decide which one you have.

Is your scooter really a moped under NC law?

Not every scooter is a moped. North Carolina defines one this way:

"A vehicle, other than a motor-driven bicycle or electric assisted bicycle, that has two or three wheels, no external shifting device, a motor that does not exceed 50 cubic centimeters piston displacement and cannot propel the vehicle at a speed greater than 30 miles per hour on a level surface. The motor may be powered by electricity, alternative fuel, motor fuel, or a combination of each."— N.C.G.S. § 20-4.01(27)j.

If your scooter's engine is over 50cc or it can go faster than 30 mph, it's a motorcycle under state law, with a title, a motorcycle endorsement and motorcycle registration.

A plate means insurance.

Registering a moped: plate, title and age

"Mopeds shall be registered with the Division. The owner of the moped shall pay the same base fee and be issued the same type of registration card and plate issued for a motorcycle."— N.C.G.S. § 20-53.4(a)
  • No title. Under § 20-53.4(b), the owner of a moped isn't required to apply for a certificate of title. To register, the moped must have a manufacturer's certificate of origin and be built for use on highways.
  • Age 16 or older. It's unlawful for anyone under 16 to ride a moped on a highway or public vehicular area (N.C.G.S. § 20-10.1).
  • No inspection or property tax. NCDMV notes mopeds need no annual inspection and owe no vehicle property tax (NCDMV vehicle registration; N.C.G.S. § 20-183.2).

Get your proof of insurance before you head to the plate agency.

Why do you need to keep coverage while the moped is registered?

Coverage has to stay active as long as the plate does. If liability insurance ends, NCDMV is notified and mails a notice to the owner. Effective October 1, 2026, you have 30 days from the date NCDMV sent the notice to respond (NCDMV insurance requirements). A real lapse can bring civil penalties, fees and plate revocation. If you're done riding, turn in the plate first, then cancel the insurance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need insurance to register a moped in North Carolina?

Yes. North Carolina's financial responsibility law says the term motor vehicle includes mopeds, so you must show proof of liability insurance to register one and keep that coverage in force the whole time it is registered. The minimum is fifty thousand dollars per person and one hundred thousand dollars per accident for bodily injury, plus fifty thousand dollars for property damage.

How do I know if my scooter is a moped or a motorcycle in North Carolina?

Under state law, a moped has two or three wheels, no external shifting device, a motor of no more than fifty cubic centimeters, and cannot go faster than thirty miles per hour on a level surface. A scooter with a bigger engine or a higher top speed is treated as a motorcycle, which needs a title, a motorcycle endorsement and its own registration.

Do I need a title or a motorcycle endorsement for a moped in North Carolina?

No title is required. The owner of a moped is not required to apply for a certificate of title, but the moped must have a manufacturer's certificate of origin to be registered. A motorcycle endorsement is not required for a moped, but you must be at least sixteen years old to ride one on a highway or public vehicular area.

Does an electric bicycle need insurance in North Carolina?

An electric assisted bicycle, as North Carolina defines it, is not registered with NCDMV and is not covered by the registration insurance requirement for motor vehicles. It must have working pedals and an electric motor of seven hundred fifty watts or less that cannot push it faster than twenty miles per hour on level ground by motor alone. If yours is faster or more powerful, it may be a moped or a motorcycle.

What happens if I cancel my moped insurance but keep the plate?

NCDMV is notified when liability coverage ends. Beginning October first, twenty twenty-six, you have thirty days from the date on NCDMV's notice to respond. If coverage really lapsed, civil penalties and fees can apply and the plate can be revoked. If you are done riding, turn in the plate before you cancel the insurance.
