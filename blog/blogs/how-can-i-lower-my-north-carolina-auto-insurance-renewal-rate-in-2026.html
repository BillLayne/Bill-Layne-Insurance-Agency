How did the 2025 North Carolina auto insurance law change affect rates?

The North Carolina Motor Vehicle Safety Responsibility Act amendment, effective July 1, 2025, increased minimum liability coverage limits to $50,000 for bodily injury per person, $100,000 per accident, and $50,000 for property damage. This was the first major increase in decades, designed to account for rising medical costs and vehicle repair prices. While this provides better protection, it automatically increased premiums for drivers who previously carried the old state minimums.

Why is my North Carolina insurance renewal increasing even with no accidents?

Rate increases occurring without accidents are often caused by general rate revisions approved by the North Carolina Rate Bureau due to inflation, higher repair costs, and increased severity of weather events. Insurance is a shared pool of risk. When a major event occurs—such as the devastation caused by Hurricane Helene in Western NC—the payouts deplete carrier reserves. To maintain solvency and the ability to pay future claims, carriers must adjust rates statewide.

The Impact of "Named Storms" and Flood

In 2026, carriers are stricter regarding weather risks.

  • Named Storm Deductibles: On homeowners policies bundled with auto, you may see deductibles for named storms ranging from 1% to 10% of the home's insured value.
  • NCIUA (Coastal Property Insurance Pool): Often called the "Beach Plan," usage has expanded as private carriers limit wind exposure.
  • The 30-Day Waiting Period: Following the flooding from Hurricane Helene, we remind all Elkin and Surry County residents that Flood Insurance (typically via FEMA/NFIP) has a mandatory 30-day waiting period before taking effect. Do not wait for a storm forecast to buy it.

What is the Consent to Rate (CTR) letter in North Carolina?

A Consent to Rate (CTR) letter is a document authorized by North Carolina law allowing insurance companies to charge premiums higher than the maximum rate cap set by the North Carolina Rate Bureau. While the Rate Bureau sets a "ceiling" for auto rates, most major carriers cannot operate profitably at that ceiling due to modern repair costs.

Signing a CTR is often mandatory to secure a policy with preferred carriers. However, if you receive a CTR letter upon renewal, it is a clear signal that you should contact Bill Layne Insurance to compare that rate against the open market.
