Buying a Used Mobile Home in NC: Insurance Checklist

What to gather before closing on a used single-wide or double-wide, so your insurance is lined up before the home moves or the keys change hands.

Quick answer

You can insure a used manufactured or mobile home in North Carolina before closing or moving it, provided you gather the title or serial number, HUD certification label (built on or after June 15, 1976), and set-up details early. Carriers also review roof age, skirting, tie-downs, and county tax moving permits before binding coverage.

Gather the paperwork early, and your insurance can be ready on closing day.

This guide is for buyers across inland North Carolina, from the mountains and Foothills to the Triad, Triangle, Charlotte and Lake Norman and the Piedmont. Used manufactured homes are a big part of housing here, and a little prep before closing makes insuring one much smoother.

Why does HUD-code vs. pre-1976 matter when buying a used mobile home?

North Carolina’s manufactured housing law (N.C.G.S. § 143-145) ties its definitions to the federal manufactured housing standards and treats units built before June 15, 1976 separately. For insurance, that date is the first dividing line:

  • Built on or after June 15, 1976. A HUD-code manufactured home. It should have a red HUD certification label on the outside of each section and a data plate inside.
  • Built before June 15, 1976. A pre-HUD mobile home. To be honest, many carriers limit or decline these, and those that do quote usually want updated roofs, heating, electrical and plumbing, plus clear photos.

For a broader look at manufactured home coverage options in North Carolina, read our NC mobile and manufactured home insurance guide.

Do you need a moving permit, and when does set-up matter?

If the used home is being moved, North Carolina requires a tax permit first. Under N.C.G.S. § 105-316.1, anyone other than a manufacturer, retailer or licensed mobile home carrier must get a permit from the county tax collector before moving a mobile home from its premises. The permit has to be “conspicuously displayed near the license tag on the rear of the mobile home” during transport. The county will also look at property taxes before issuing it (UNC School of Government).

Two insurance points to plan around:

  • The move. Coverage during transport is optional and, per the Department of Insurance, “must be purchased prior to the transportation of your home” (NCDOI manufactured homes guide).
  • The set-up. Carriers generally want the home permanently set up, anchored with tie-downs and skirted at its new location before standard coverage takes effect. Plan your effective date around the set-up.

Know which policy form you’re getting before you compare prices.

What’s the difference between MH(F) and MH(C) mobile home policies?

North Carolina’s mobile homeowners program uses two main forms. The Department of Insurance says they’re similar, but “differences in perils covered and coverage limitations exist.”

  • MH(F), the broader form. Covers a longer list of perils, adding collapse, weight of ice, snow or sleet, sudden tearing or bursting of heating, cooling or water-heating systems, and accidental water discharge or freezing of plumbing. It pays additional living expenses up to the limit on your declarations page.
  • MH(C), the named-perils form. Covers a shorter list of perils, plus loss while the home is being transported. Its loss-of-use benefit is limited to $10 per day for up to 60 days, according to the NCDOI guide.
  • Open-perils options. Some policies can cover the home on an open-perils basis, meaning any direct physical damage is covered unless it’s excluded.

Every form includes the core parts of a mobile home policy:

  • Dwelling and other structures: the home, attached structures, and sheds or garages on the lot.
  • Personal property: your furniture, clothes and belongings.
  • Loss of use: help with living costs if a covered loss makes the home unlivable.
  • Personal liability and medical payments: protection if someone is hurt on your property.

Physical damage is settled at actual cash value unless you buy replacement cost coverage. Ask which you’re getting.

Line up the binder and mortgagee details before closing day.

Step by step: from contract to closing

  1. Gather the checklist items as soon as you’re under contract.
  2. Send photos of all four sides, the roof, skirting, tie-downs, steps and handrails, and the HUD label or data plate.
  3. Get the move permitted with the county tax collector if the home is being relocated.
  4. Compare carriers. Among our appointed carriers, Foremost and National General both offer manufactured home coverage, and each has its own age, set-up and location rules.
  5. Confirm the lender details. Get the exact mortgagee wording and maximum deductible from your lender.
  6. Bind before closing once the home’s set-up and the effective date are confirmed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I insure a used mobile home in North Carolina before I close or move it?

Yes. You can start a quote as soon as you have the title or serial number, the year built and the address where the home will sit. Coverage for the move itself is a separate, optional coverage that has to be bought before the home is transported. Carriers usually want the home set up, tied down and skirted at its new location before standard coverage takes effect, so plan your effective date with our team.

What is the difference between a HUD-code manufactured home and a pre-1976 mobile home for insurance?

Homes built on or after June 15, 1976 were built to the federal HUD Code and carry a HUD certification label and data plate. Homes built before that date are pre-HUD mobile homes. Many carriers limit or decline pre-1976 homes, and those that accept them often require updated roofs, heating, electrical and plumbing, plus clear photos, before they will quote.

Do I need a moving permit to move a used mobile home in North Carolina?

Usually, yes. Under North Carolina law, anyone other than a manufacturer, retailer or licensed mobile home carrier needs a tax permit from the county tax collector before moving a mobile home from its premises. The permit must be displayed near the license tag on the rear of the home during transport. The county will typically ask about property taxes before it issues the permit.

What is the difference between the MH(F) and MH(C) mobile homeowners forms?

Both forms list the perils they cover, but the MH(F) form covers more of them. MH(F) adds perils such as collapse, weight of ice, snow or sleet, and accidental water discharge or freezing of plumbing, and it pays additional living expenses up to the limit on your declarations page. The MH(C) form has a shorter list and a smaller daily loss of use benefit. Some carriers also offer broader open perils options.

Is it harder to insure a mobile home on a rented park lot?

It can be. Some carriers have tighter rules for homes on rented lots in mobile home parks, and some ask about the park, the home's age and how it is set up before they quote. A home on your own land often has more carrier options. Tell our team where the home will sit, and we will check which carriers fit before you close.
