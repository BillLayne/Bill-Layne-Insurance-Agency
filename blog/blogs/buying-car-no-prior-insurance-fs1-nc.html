Can you buy a car in NC with no active insurance policy?

Yes. Lots of people buy a car without a current policy: first-time buyers, people coming off a family member’s policy, people who sold their last car, and people moving here from another state. You don’t need prior insurance to buy. You need an owner’s policy on this car before it’s registered.

Our agency insures cars you own, finance or lease. We do not offer standalone non-owner policies or DL-123-only policies. If your old coverage ended and you’re dealing with an NCDMV notice, start with our guide to car insurance after a lapse in NC.

What does North Carolina law require to register a car?

“No motor vehicle shall be registered in this State unless the owner at the time of registration provides proof of financial responsibility for the operation of such motor vehicle, as provided in this Article.”— N.C.G.S. § 20-309(a)
  • Proof format. Under § 20-309(c1), proof of insurance can be in physical or electronic format, including images shown on a phone from the insurer’s app or website.
  • An NC-licensed company. NCDMV requires liability insurance from a company licensed to do business in North Carolina, and says plainly that out-of-state policies are not accepted (NCDMV insurance requirements).
  • Minimum limits. For policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, the minimum is 50/100/50 liability plus uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage (N.C.G.S. § 20-279.21).
  • Form FS-1. North Carolina’s certificate of insurance. Your insurer provides it once the policy is bound, and it lists the car by VIN.

Cash car or financed car: the lender’s rules add coverage.

Cash purchase vs. financed or leased: what coverage do you need?

  • Paid in full. The state minimum is 50/100/50 liability plus UM/UIM. Comprehensive and Collision are optional, so you choose based on the car’s value and your budget.
  • Financed or leased. The lender or leasing company typically requires Comprehensive and Collision, subject to your contract’s maximum deductible and loss payee listing. Bring the lender’s name and address. Our guide to financed or leased car insurance requirements in NC covers the details.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy a car in North Carolina if I do not have car insurance right now?

Yes. You do not need an existing policy to buy a car. You need to bind an owner's policy on the specific car you are buying before it is registered. Once the policy is bound, your North Carolina licensed insurer can provide Form FS-1 when needed so the dealership or plate agency can register the car in your name.

What is Form FS-1 and who provides it?

Form FS-1 is North Carolina's certificate of insurance. It shows NCDMV that the vehicle has liability coverage from a company licensed to do business in North Carolina. Your insurance company or agent provides it after your policy is bound, and it lists the specific vehicle by its VIN.

Can I use an out-of-state insurance policy to register a car in North Carolina?

No. NCDMV requires liability insurance from a company licensed to do business in North Carolina, and it states that out-of-state policies are not accepted. If you are moving here or switching from another state's policy, you need a North Carolina policy before you register the car.

Do I need Comprehensive and Collision if I pay cash for the car?

Not by law. North Carolina requires liability limits of at least fifty, one hundred, fifty plus uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. If you pay cash and own the car outright, adding Comprehensive and Collision is your choice. If you finance or lease, your lender or leasing company typically requires both, subject to your contract's maximum deductible and loss payee listing.

Does your agency write non-owner or DL-123 only policies?

No. Our agency insures cars you own, finance or lease. We do not offer standalone non-owner policies or policies written only to file a DL-123. If you are buying a car, we can quote an owner's policy on that vehicle and provide Form FS-1 once it is bound.
