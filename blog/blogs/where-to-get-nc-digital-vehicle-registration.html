Where to Get Your NC Digital Vehicle Registration (2026): Free PDF Link, Title Number Lookup & $5 Paper Copy

Renewed your tags after October 1 and didn’t get a registration card or plate sticker? Here is the exact NCDMV link to download your free PDF registration card, how to find the 5-digit title number the portal asks for, and how to order a $5 paper card if you still want one in the glovebox.

By Bill Layne, licensed NC agent since 2005Published October 19, 20267 min read

Quick answer: Where to go right now

You do not need to carry a printed registration card in North Carolina anymore—law enforcement verifies your registration electronically by your license plate. If you want a digital or printed copy for out-of-state trips, car rentals, or your glovebox, go to the official NCDMV Digital Vehicle Registration page, enter your License Plate Number and the last 5 digits of your Title Number, and download the free PDF. Prefer an official DMV-printed copy? You can order one online via myNCDMV or pick one up at a local License Plate Agency for $5.

Starting October 1, 2026, the North Carolina Division of Motor Vehicles retired printed registration cards and license plate stickers under Senate Bill 257. Now that October renewals are in full swing around Elkin, Mount Airy, Wilkesboro, and Yadkinville, the number-one question we hear at the agency isn’t why the state made the switch—it’s “Where do I actually go to get my digital registration?”

Even more common: drivers open the NCDMV website, see that it asks for the last 5 digits of their vehicle’s title number, and get stuck because the title is locked in a safe or held electronically by their auto lender.

Here is the exact step-by-step walkthrough—with direct links to every official NCDMV tool—so you can save your digital registration to your phone or print a copy at home in under two minutes.

Step-by-Step: How to Download Your Free NC Digital Registration PDF

NCDMV updates your electronic registration record within a couple of minutes after you renew online, at a kiosk, or at a license plate agency. To download your official PDF copy:

  1. Open the official NCDMV portal: Visit NCDMV’s Get Your Digital Vehicle Registration page.
  2. Enter your License Plate Number: Type your current North Carolina tag number (no spaces or dashes needed).
  3. Enter the Last 5 Digits of Your Title Number: Type the final five numbers of your 15-digit NC vehicle title number (see the next section if you don’t have your title in front of you).
  4. Save or print the free PDF: Once your PDF opens, save it to the Files or Apple Books app on your iPhone, the Files by Google app on Android, or print a copy on your home printer for your glovebox.

Trouble downloading your PDF? If the NCDMV lookup tool gives you an error or won’t load your PDF after a recent renewal, NCDMV advises calling their Vehicle Registration helpline at 919-715-7000 (weekdays, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.) or submitting a request through the NCDMV Vehicle Registration contact form.

Where Do You Find the Last 5 Digits of Your Title Number?

If your car or truck is financed, the bank holds the title electronically—so you won’t have a paper title in a desk drawer. Fortunately, you don’t need the physical title certificate to find the number. NCDMV gives you three easy places to spot it:

1. Your Tag & Tax Together Renewal Notice (Fastest)
Look at the top vehicle-details box on the registration renewal notice NCDMV mailed or emailed you 90 days before expiration. The Title Number is printed right beside your VIN and Plate Number. You can view NCDMV’s official sample Tag & Tax notice (PDF) to see the exact box location.
2. Your Online MyDMV Account
Threw away the paper renewal notice? Log in to NCDMV’s MyDMV Portal. Under your active vehicles list, select your vehicle to view the full title number, then use the last 5 digits on the digital registration download page.
3. An Old Registration Card or Recent DMV Receipt
If you still have last year’s paper registration card in your glovebox—or the receipt from your most recent online, kiosk, or license plate agency renewal—the title number is printed on that document as well.

How Do NC Safety Inspections, Stickers & Traffic Stops Work Now?

Even though the paper card and plate sticker are gone, your annual obligations haven’t changed:

  • What to do with old plate stickers: NCDMV officially recommends removing old registration stickers from your license plate on or after October 1, 2026.
  • Never hand your phone to police for registration: NCDMV states clearly that you will never be asked to hand over your phone to a police officer to verify registration. In North Carolina, officers check your registration status on their own patrol computer using your license plate number.
  • Out-of-state road trips: Digital registration is accepted in most states, but out-of-state officers may not have direct access to NC’s database. Before driving across the line into Virginia, Tennessee, or South Carolina, save the free PDF on your phone or slip a printed copy into your glovebox.
  • Safety & emissions inspections: You still must pass an NC safety inspection (and emissions inspection in the 19 required counties) within 90 days before renewing. Inspection technicians now look up your vehicle using the barcode on your digital registration PDF, your Tag & Tax property notice, or any recent DMV transaction receipt.

The Catch: Digital Registration Does NOT Replace Your Insurance Card

Don’t toss your insurance card! NCDMV’s digital switch applies only to your vehicle registration card and plate sticker. You must still carry separate proof of auto liability insurance (either a paper ID card or inside your insurance carrier’s mobile app), and your vehicle must maintain continuous NC liability coverage every single day the plate is active.

In fact, moving to an all-electronic registration database makes continuous auto insurance even more important. Because law enforcement and NCDMV rely entirely on the live electronic record:

  • Zero buffer for an insurance lapse: If your auto policy cancels for non-payment or you cancel coverage before turning in your plate, your carrier notifies NCDMV electronically under G.S. 20-309. That triggers a Notice of Termination of Liability Insurance (Form FS-5), civil penalties of $50 to $150, and potential 30-day plate revocation.
  • Selling or parking a vehicle? Always turn in your NC license plate at a License Plate Agency before you cancel the insurance.
  • Check your 2026 NC minimum limits: Every North Carolina auto policy issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, carries at least 50/100/50 liability limits ($50,000 per person / $100,000 per accident bodily injury and $50,000 property damage) plus Underinsured Motorist coverage.

2-Minute NC Digital Registration Checklist

Do these four steps right after your next tag renewal

  • Download the free PDF: Visit the NCDMV Digital Registration page with your plate number and last 5 digits of your title number.
  • Save a backup for out-of-state trips: Store the PDF on your phone and/or print one copy for your glovebox alongside your auto insurance ID card.
  • Peel off your old plate sticker: Remove expired registration stickers from your license plate so your tag stays clean and legible.
  • Set a calendar reminder for next year: Without a colored sticker on your bumper, make sure your email address is linked in myNCDMV so you receive the 90-day renewal reminder.

Frequently Asked Questions: Getting Your NC Digital Registration

Where do I go to download my North Carolina digital vehicle registration?

Go directly to the official NCDMV Digital Vehicle Registration page. Enter your license plate number and the last 5 digits of your vehicle’s title number to download a free PDF copy that you can save to your phone or print at home.

Where can I find the last 5 digits of my title number if my car is financed?

You do not need the physical paper title. The title number is printed on your annual Tag & Tax Together registration renewal notice, on last year’s registration card or renewal receipt, and inside your online MyDMV account.

How much does a printed registration card cost from NCDMV?

Printing the downloaded PDF yourself at home is completely free. If you want an official printed registration card from NCDMV, it costs $5 either in person at a North Carolina License Plate Agency or ordered online by mail through myNCDMV.

Can I add my NC vehicle registration to Apple Wallet or Google Wallet right now?

Not directly as a native pass yet. NCDMV announced that native integration inside the official NC Wallet (alongside the NC Mobile ID) is scheduled for 2027. Until then, saving the free PDF to your phone’s Files or Books app is the official way to carry it on your device.

Will a police officer ask me to hand over my phone at a traffic stop?

No. NCDMV confirms you will never be asked to hand over your phone to law enforcement to verify your registration. Officers verify North Carolina registration electronically using their patrol car’s computer system.
