Which NC rating territory is your Triad home in: 310, 320 or 280?

The North Carolina Rate Bureau sets homeowners base rates by territory, and territories follow county lines. Most of the Triad is in Territory 310. Davidson and Randolph counties are in Territory 320. Only the Orange County side of Mebane reaches Territory 280. Here is how they compare, using the Department of Insurance's published data.

Why can two High Point or Archdale neighbors land in different territories?

High Point is the only North Carolina city that spans four counties: Guilford, Forsyth, Randolph and Davidson. Guilford and Forsyth are Territory 310. Randolph and Davidson are Territory 320. So two homes a few streets apart can start from different base rates. Archdale and Trinity sit near the same Randolph–Guilford line, and Kernersville straddles Forsyth and Guilford (both 310, but different county parcel records).

Why did your Triad renewal go up if statewide rates are set until 2027?

On January 17, 2025, the Department of Insurance and the Rate Bureau settled the last homeowners rate request. Statewide base rates rose an average of 7.5 percent on June 1, 2025 and another 7.5 percent on June 1, 2026, with no territory above 35 percent, and the Rate Bureau agreed not to seek another increase before June 1, 2027 (NCDOI announcement). In Territory 310, the approved changes were 8.2 percent and 8.3 percent.

That settlement controls the base rate, not the price a single carrier charges you. Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-30, a carrier can charge more than the base rate through Consent to Rate, and since 2019 you don't have to sign anything for it to apply. Roughly 69 of every 100 Territory 310 homeowners policies carried a Consent to Rate surcharge in 2025. Learn how it works in our NC Consent to Rate guide and see territory numbers in our NC Consent to Rate data by county.

How do hail, wind and big oaks affect Triad homeowners coverage?

Neighborhoods like Irving Park, Fisher Park and Lindley Park in Greensboro, Ardmore, Buena Vista and Sherwood Forest in Winston-Salem, and Emerywood in High Point have big hardwoods and older homes. Piedmont hail and straight-line winds hit every spring and summer. Here is what to check:

  • Wind and hail deductibles. Some policies use a flat deductible, for example $1,000 or $2,500. Others use a percentage of your dwelling coverage (Coverage A), for example 1 or 2 percent, which can be much larger on a roof claim.
  • Roof settlement. Some policies pay roof claims at replacement cost. Others use an actual cash value roof schedule that pays less as the roof ages. Ask which one your quote uses, especially if your roof is past 15 years.
  • Fallen trees. A tree that falls on your house or garage in a storm is generally covered. Removing a tree that hits nothing is often limited.
  • Water backup and service lines. Backups through drains or sumps are usually excluded unless you add water backup coverage. Service line coverage helps with buried water, sewer and electric lines on your property.
  • Replacement cost on belongings. Make sure your personal property is written at replacement cost, not actual cash value.

Are NC minimum auto limits enough for I-40, I-85 and US-52 commuters?

For policies written or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, North Carolina requires at least 50/100/50 liability limits under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-279.21. That is the legal floor. A multi-car pileup on I-40, I-85, Salem Parkway, I-73, I-74 or US-52 can run well past it, and you could owe the difference.

  • Higher limits. Many commuters choose limits such as 100/300/100, with matching uninsured and underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage and medical payments.
  • SDIP points. North Carolina's Safe Driver Incentive Plan (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-65) adds surcharges for at-fault accidents and moving violations, generally for three policy years.
  • October 1, 2026 auto update. For new and renewal personal auto policies effective on or after October 1, 2026, updated North Carolina Personal Auto Program forms narrow the general delivery-of-goods liability exclusion so it applies only above state minimum limits, update repair-shop choice disclosures and low-speed vehicle definitions without a rate-level change, and keep built-in Part D transportation expenses in the revised Amendatory Endorsement at up to $30 per day ($900 maximum) with no deductible (NCRF Circular RF-26-7, adopting NCRB Circular A-26-2).

What do Triad renters, landlords and manufactured home owners need?

  • Renters (HO-4). Students and staff renting near Wake Forest, UNCG, NC A&T, High Point University and Elon need their own renters policy. Your landlord's policy doesn't cover your belongings or your liability. Start a renters quote.
  • Landlords (DP-3). Rental homes need a dwelling policy (DP-3), not a homeowners policy. We compare landlord options through Steadily, Foremost, Travelers and NC Grange Mutual. Dwelling base rates rise an average of 5 percent statewide on October 1, 2026 and again on October 1, 2027, varying by territory (NCDOI announcement).
  • Manufactured homes and rural acreage. In Davidson, Randolph, Stokes, Davie and Rockingham counties, many families own a manufactured home on their own land. Foremost and National General both offer manufactured and mobile home options, and we can compare them side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which homeowners rating territory covers Greensboro, Winston-Salem and High Point?

Greensboro and Winston-Salem, along with the rest of Guilford, Forsyth, Alamance, Davie, Rockingham, Stokes and Surry counties, are in North Carolina homeowners Territory 310. Davidson and Randolph counties, including Lexington, Thomasville, Asheboro and Archdale, are in Territory 320. High Point falls in both, depending on which county your home sits in.

Why can two High Point neighbors have different home insurance rates?

High Point is the only North Carolina city that spans four counties: Guilford, Forsyth, Randolph and Davidson. Homeowners rating territories follow county lines, so a home on the Guilford or Forsyth side rates in Territory 310, while a home on the Randolph or Davidson side rates in Territory 320. Each carrier then adds its own underwriting, so neighbors can see different prices.

Why did my Triad home insurance renewal go up if statewide rates are set until 2027?

The January 2025 settlement set statewide base rate increases for June 2025 and June 2026 and kept the Rate Bureau from seeking another increase before June 1, 2027. That limits the base rate, not what an individual carrier charges. A carrier can still charge more than the base rate through Consent to Rate, and roof age, claims and coverage changes also move your price.

Does Bill Layne Insurance offer NC Grange Mutual and Alamance Farmers' Mutual in the Triad?

Yes. NC Grange Mutual is headquartered in Greensboro, and Alamance Farmers' Mutual has been based in Graham since 1892. We quote both alongside Nationwide, Travelers, Progressive, National General, Foremost and Universal Property. NC Grange Mutual does not use credit-based scoring on qualifying homeowners policies, subject to roof age and eligibility.

What changed in North Carolina auto policies on October 1, 2026?

For new and renewal personal auto policies effective on or after October 1, 2026, North Carolina Rate Bureau and Reinsurance Facility form updates narrow the general delivery-of-goods liability exclusion so it applies only above state minimum limits, update repair-shop choice disclosures and low-speed vehicle definitions without a rate-level change, and keep the Amendatory Endorsement's built-in Part D transportation expense benefit at up to thirty dollars per day, to a maximum of nine hundred dollars, with no deductible.
