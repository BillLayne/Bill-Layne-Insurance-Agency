NC DL-123: Check Your Insurance Proof Before the DMV Visit

Bill Layne Insurance Agency does not write non-owner policies. For documents on an existing policy, contact your current insurer. Updated September 11, 2026.

Before a license appointment, check which insurance documents NCDMV accepts and make sure your proof is current. A DL-123 is one option; a qualifying insurance card can also be accepted.

What Is a DL-123?

A DL-123 is an insurance certificate provided by a North Carolina insurer. NCDMV also accepts qualifying policies, insurance cards, binders, and insurer letters. The applicant must be listed on the proof.

Check the official NCDMV insurance-proof requirements and any specific instructions for your license or court-related transaction.

Check Dates Before Your Appointment

Ask your current insurer to confirm the issue date, validity, and delivery time for the document you need. Request corrections before your visit. Document preparation time varies; do not rely on an instant or same-day promise.

If You Do Not Own a Car

Not owning a car does not automatically establish eligibility for non-owner insurance. Household vehicles and regular access matter. Ask the insurer covering any vehicle you drive about the correct driver listing and coverage.

Bill Layne Insurance Agency does not write non-owner policies. If you need that coverage, consult a provider that offers it and ask about eligibility and required documents.

Keep Coverage and Paperwork Separate

An insurance certificate does not keep a policy active. Before changing or cancelling coverage, confirm your obligations with your insurer and NCDMV. Follow any license-restoration or court instructions that apply to your situation.

Common Questions About the DL-123

Q: Can I show the DL-123 on my phone?

A: NCDMV accepts several forms of insurance proof. You can bring printed documents or securely upload digital copies during your driver license office visit. Follow the office instructions for uploading; simply displaying a phone screen is not the same process.

Q: Does the name on the DL-123 matter?

A: The applicant must be listed on the proof. Check the name and policy information with your insurer and resolve discrepancies before the appointment.

Q: How much does a DL-123 cost?

A: Ask your current insurer about document availability and any costs. Do not assume you need to purchase another policy just to obtain proof of coverage you already have.
