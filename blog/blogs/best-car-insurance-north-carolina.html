What is North Carolina's mandatory 2025–2026 statutory floor? (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-279.21)

For policies issued or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, North Carolina requires at least 50/100/50 liability limits under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-279.21. Before that date the minimums were lower.

Liability limitMinimum
Bodily injury, per person$50,000
Bodily injury, per accident$100,000
Property damage, per accident$50,000

The same statute requires uninsured motorist (UM) coverage for bodily injury and property damage and underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage for bodily injury on policies covering NC vehicles. Unless you choose different limits, UM and UIM bodily injury default to the highest bodily injury liability limit on the policy, and you cannot go below the required minimums.

Minimums are a floor, not a plan. These optional coverages are worth a look:

  • Medical payments. Helps with medical bills for you and your passengers after a crash, regardless of fault.
  • Comprehensive. Pays for damage that is not a collision, such as theft, hail, a falling tree, or hitting a deer. Deer collisions are a real concern on NC back roads.
  • Collision. Pays to repair or replace your vehicle after a crash with another vehicle or object.

Your driving record follows you across every county, from the mountains to the coast.

How do NC SDIP points and the inexperienced-operator change affect your carrier choice?

The Safe Driver Incentive Plan (SDIP) is North Carolina's system of surcharges and discounts based on your driving record. It is set out in N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-65. A few points that matter when you shop:

  • How long surcharges last. Under the statute, an SDIP surcharge runs three policy years. Convictions carrying four or more points run five policy years, with an exception for some speeding convictions.
  • Inexperienced operators. For drivers first licensed before July 1, 2025, the plan may surcharge those with less than three years of licensed experience. For drivers first licensed on or after July 1, 2025, that window grows to eight years.
  • Inexperienced safe driver discount. After three full years of experience with no at-fault accidents or convictions, a discount may apply. For this discount, a conviction includes one where a prayer for judgment continued (PJC) was granted.
  • Why it matters for shopping. The statutory surcharges apply across standard policies, but carriers differ on optional coverages, safe-driver programs, and multi-policy credits.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which car insurance company is best in North Carolina?

No single carrier is best for every driver across all one hundred North Carolina counties. Nationwide and Travelers often fit homeowners who bundle auto and property policies with clean driving records, Progressive and National General offer broad flexibility for multi-driver households, Foremost pairs well with manufactured homes and recreational vehicles, and The General helps drivers who need immediate state filings. Comparing all six appointed carriers side by side shows which company fits your specific household and coverage needs.

What are the minimum car insurance requirements in North Carolina for 2026?

Under North Carolina General Statute section twenty dash two hundred seventy-nine point twenty-one (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-279.21), every auto policy issued or renewed in North Carolina must carry at least fifty thousand dollars in bodily injury liability per person, one hundred thousand dollars in bodily injury liability per accident, and fifty thousand dollars in property damage liability per accident, along with matching uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage.

Why should I use an independent insurance agency instead of quoting one company online?

A single-brand insurer can only show you its own underwriting rules and single price. An independent North Carolina agency compares six appointed auto carriers and eight homeowners carriers from one conversation, checks your eligibility for multi-policy and safe-driver discounts, and serves as your dedicated local advocate if you ever have a claim or billing question.

How do North Carolina SDIP insurance points affect which carrier I should choose?

North Carolina uses the Safe Driver Incentive Plan under General Statute section fifty-eight dash thirty-six dash sixty-five (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-65) to assign surcharge points for at-fault accidents and moving violations over a three-year lookback period. While statutory SDIP surcharges apply across standard policies, carriers differ in how they underwrite optional coverages, safe-driver programs, and multi-policy credits.
