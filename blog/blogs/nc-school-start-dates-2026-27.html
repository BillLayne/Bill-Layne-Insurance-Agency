Nine days from now, yellow buses are back on Highway 21, US 601 and every two-lane road between Elkin and Traphill — unless you live in Wilkes County, where they are not. Our five local districts could not agree on a start date this year, and the gap is almost two weeks wide. Here is every verified date, why the boards split, and the one first-week mistake that follows a driver in these parts for five years.

Do this before the buses roll on August 12

Start here. Five things worth doing this week — before the first morning rush at Elkin Elementary, Dobson, Boonville or Millers Creek, not after it.

  1. Find your district’s first day and lock it in

    Surry, Yadkin and Elkin City start Wednesday, August 12. Mount Airy City starts Monday, August 17. Wilkes County starts Monday, August 24. Put the right one in your phone with a reminder the Friday before.

  2. Drive the route once before day one

    Run the trip at the actual time you’ll be making it. Sun angle, backed-up traffic at the school entrance and the spot where a bus stops on a blind curve all look different at 7:15 a.m. than they do on a Saturday.

  3. Read the stopped-bus rule out loud to every driver in the house

    On a two-lane road, traffic in both directions must stop for a school bus with its stop arm out. Teenagers, grandparents helping with pickup, anyone who drives your cars — everybody needs to hear it before the buses roll.

  4. Add the new driver before the first trip to school, not after

    A newly licensed driver has to be listed on the policy. Call before the first solo drive so coverage and limits are settled, instead of finding out how it works during a claim.

  5. Ask for the student and driver-training discounts by name

    Good-student and driver-education credits are not always applied automatically. Ask which ones your carrier offers and what report card or certificate it needs to see.

None of that takes an afternoon. The first week of school is the one week a year when traffic patterns change overnight in every town from Ronda to Hays, and it is also the week new drivers make their first unsupervised trip anywhere.

Know your own district’s date, drive the route once, and settle the teen driver on your policy before the first bell — not after something happens.

Why Wilkes starts 12 days after Surry

This is the question I hear most, and the answer is not a clerical error. North Carolina’s school calendar law tells traditional-calendar districts to open no earlier than the Monday closest to August 26. A lot of boards across the state have decided the law does not serve their students, and they vote for an earlier start anyway.

Surry County did exactly that. The board voted 5-0 to start August 12, knowing it conflicted with state law, after a survey in which 83.3% of 369 respondents backed the earlier date. Board member Tony Hutchens put it plainly: “We are serving the students of Surry County.” The driver is dual enrollment — more than 1,500 high schoolers take classes through Surry Community College, and the college does not wait until late August.

Elkin City Schools approved the same August 12 start on February 23, 2026, for the same reasons: aligning with the community college, and finishing first-semester exams before Christmas break rather than after it. Board chair Jennifer Kleinheksel noted the overwhelming majority of survey responses favored the earlier date.

Wilkes County went the other way. Its board approved the calendar that complies with the statute — August 24 to May 28 — even though members had said they preferred the earlier option that would have wrapped exams before the holidays. The compromise is Wilkes Early College High School, which sits on the Wilkes Community College campus and starts August 12 with the college.

📖 In plain English

Dual enrollment — high school students taking real college courses at Surry Community College or Wilkes Community College for credit. When the district calendar and the college calendar do not line up, those students start two schools on two different days.

The gap is a policy disagreement, not a mistake: three boards prioritized the community college calendar, one prioritized the statute.

What passing a stopped bus really costs

Now the part that is actually my job. On the roads around Pilot Mountain, State Road, Lowgap and East Bend, a school bus stops where the child lives — on a curve, at the bottom of a hill, on a road with no shoulder. Buses make those stops twice a day, roughly 180 days a year, starting next week.

Passing one while its stop arm is out is not a routine traffic ticket. A conviction is a Class 1 misdemeanor with a minimum $500 fine and five DMV points — and 12 DMV points in three years suspends a North Carolina license. If a child is struck, the charge escalates to a felony.

Then the insurance side arrives, and it is a separate scoreboard. Under North Carolina’s Safe Driver Incentive Plan, passing a stopped school bus carries four insurance points, which the rate schedule commonly translates to a surcharge around 80%.

Here is the change most drivers have not heard about. The Department of Insurance states that for convictions assigned four or more SDIP points, other than speeding, occurring on or after July 1, 2025, the surcharge period is five policy years — it used to be three. On a $1,600-a-year auto policy, an 80% surcharge is roughly $1,280 more per year. Across five years, that single decision can total more than $6,000 in premium, depending on your policy and carrier.

School zones are carved out of the forgiveness rule

North Carolina normally waives insurance points for speeding 10 mph or less over the limit when the speed is under 55 mph and you have a clean three-year record. That waiver does not apply to school zones. Every activated 20-mph zone in Elkin, Jonesville, Mount Airy, Dobson and Wilkesboro is therefore a place where a small ticket behaves like a big one — and the full cost of an NC speeding ticket is rarely the fine on the citation.

One stopped-bus conviction can outlast a car loan: five DMV points, four insurance points, and a surcharge that now runs five policy years.

The teen driver in your driveway

For a lot of families in Elkin, Jonesville and Yadkinville, August 12 is not just the first day of school — it is the first day a sixteen-year-old drives themselves to it. That is a bigger insurance event than most parents expect, and the timing matters.

Three things are worth knowing before that first solo trip:

  • A newly licensed driver has to go on the policy. A learner’s permit is usually handled differently than a license — once they are licensed, they are a rated driver in your household. Taking one back off later is its own trap: under the continuous-coverage rule, a gap can cost a teen their license.
  • Inexperience is priced, and not just for a few months. North Carolina rates a new driver as inexperienced for years after licensing. Good-student and driver-training credits are the practical offset, and a clean record protects eligibility for the newer inexperienced-safe-driver discount.
  • Minimum limits are thin protection. North Carolina’s required liability limits are now 50/100/50. A newer pickup in a school parking lot can exceed the property damage limit by itself, and a serious injury claim can pass $50,000 quickly. This is the wrong coverage to buy at the legal floor.

Which vehicle the teen is assigned to matters too. So does whether the car they drive to Starmount, Forbush, East Wilkes or North Surry carries collision coverage worth having. Those are five-minute conversations that change the number on your bill more than shopping alone does.

Call before the first drive to school, ask for the student and training discounts by name, and do not leave a new driver on minimum liability limits.

Frequently asked questions

When does school start in Surry County for 2026-27?
Wednesday, August 12, 2026. The Surry County Board of Education voted 5-0 in early 2026 to keep the early August start after 83.3% of survey respondents backed it, even though state law sets the opening date no earlier than the Monday closest to August 26.
When does school start in Wilkes County for 2026-27?
Monday, August 24, 2026, with the last day on Friday, May 28, 2027. The Wilkes board approved the calendar that complies with state law, which puts Wilkes students back in class 12 days after their neighbors in Surry, Yadkin and Elkin. Wilkes Early College High School is the exception — it starts Wednesday, August 12 to stay on the Wilkes Community College schedule.
When does school start in Yadkin County for 2026-27?
Wednesday, August 12, 2026, running to Friday, May 21, 2027 — 172 student days on the calendar the Yadkin County Board of Education adopted February 2, 2026. Thanksgiving break is November 23-27, students are out for Christmas after December 18 and return January 6, and spring break is March 8-12, 2027.
Why do Surry and Wilkes start almost two weeks apart?
North Carolina law tells traditional-calendar districts to open no earlier than the Monday closest to August 26. Surry, Yadkin and Elkin City adopted earlier starts anyway, mostly to line up with Surry Community College dual enrollment and to finish first-semester exams before Christmas. Wilkes chose the compliant calendar instead. Both decisions were made in public board votes.
What happens if I pass a stopped school bus in North Carolina?
It is a serious charge, not a routine ticket. A conviction carries a Class 1 misdemeanor and a minimum $500 fine, five DMV points toward license suspension, and four insurance points under the Safe Driver Incentive Plan — commonly around an 80% surcharge. If a child is struck, the charge escalates to a felony.
How long does a school bus violation stay on my insurance?
Longer than it used to. The North Carolina Department of Insurance says that for convictions assigned four or more SDIP points, other than speeding, occurring on or after July 1, 2025, the surcharge period is five policy years instead of three.
When can I keep driving while a school bus is stopped?
On a two-lane road, everybody stops — both directions. On a divided highway with a median or physical barrier, traffic behind the bus must stop while oncoming traffic generally may proceed. Center turn lanes and undivided multi-lane roads trip people up, so check NCDOT’s stopped-bus chart, and when you are not sure, stop.
Will adding my teenager double our car insurance?
It often costs less than parents fear, but a newly licensed driver is rated as inexperienced for several years and that shows up in the premium. What helps: good-student and driver-training credits, which car the teen is assigned to, and a clean record — a conviction can block the newer discount for inexperienced safe drivers.
