Financed or Leased Car Insurance Requirements in NC

What North Carolina law requires, what your lender or leasing company adds, and how to have your proof ready before you sign.

What does North Carolina law require on any car?

Before any car can get a North Carolina plate, the owner has to prove insurance. The law says it plainly:

"No motor vehicle shall be registered in this State unless the owner at the time of registration provides proof of financial responsibility for the operation of such motor vehicle, as provided in this Article. The owner of each motor vehicle registered in this State shall maintain financial responsibility continuously throughout the period of registration."— N.C.G.S. § 20-309(a)

Leased cars are no exception. North Carolina treats a car leased to a resident as a car that has to be registered here:

"A vehicle that is leased to an individual who is a resident of this State is a vehicle intended to be operated upon a highway of this State."— N.C.G.S. § 20-50(a)

For policies written or renewed on or after July 1, 2025, the required minimum is 50/100/50 liability: $50,000 per person and $100,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $50,000 for property damage, under N.C.G.S. § 20-279.21. The same statute requires uninsured and underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage. That's the legal floor. It does not include Comprehensive or Collision, which pay to fix or replace your own car.

What do auto lenders require on a financed car in NC?

When you finance, the lender holds a lien on the title until the loan is paid off. To protect that interest, most loan agreements require:

  • Comprehensive and Collision for as long as you owe money. People often call this "full coverage," but it isn't a legal term. It just means liability plus physical damage coverage on your own car.
  • A maximum deductible. The contract sets a cap on your Comprehensive and Collision deductibles. Many cap it at $1,000, and some at $500. Check yours before you choose.
  • A lienholder (loss payee) clause. The lender is listed on your policy so a total loss check includes them and they're notified if coverage lapses.

What do leasing companies require in NC?

With a lease, the leasing company owns the car for the whole term, so it usually asks for more:

  • Higher liability limits. Many leases require limits well above 50/100/50. The exact numbers are in your lease.
  • Lower deductibles. Leases often cap Comprehensive and Collision deductibles lower than auto loans do.
  • Loss payee and additional insured. The leasing company is usually listed both ways on your policy.

Under N.C.G.S. § 20-50(a) and NCDMV vehicle registration rules, a vehicle leased to a North Carolina resident must carry North Carolina financial responsibility and proper state registration.

If you owe more than the car is worth, a total loss can leave a gap.

Loan/lease payoff coverage or a dealer GAP waiver?

New cars lose value fast. If your car is totaled while you owe more than it's worth, Collision pays the car's actual cash value, not your loan balance. Two products can help close that gap:

  • Loan/lease payoff coverage is added to your auto policy by some carriers. It helps pay the difference between the car's value and what you owe, up to the policy's limit.
  • A dealer GAP waiver is sold by the dealership as part of your financing. It's tied to your loan rather than your insurance policy.

Many leases already include GAP protection, so check before you buy it twice. For what happens to your loan after a total loss, read how an NC total loss works when you still owe on the car.

What happens if you drop coverage before the loan is paid off?

It's tempting to drop Comprehensive or Collision to lower your premium, but while you still owe on the car, that usually breaks your loan agreement. The lender is notified through the lienholder clause and can buy collateral protection insurance and add the cost to your loan. That coverage protects the lender, not you. Keep physical damage coverage until the loan is paid off and the lien is released, then talk with our team about whether to keep it.

  1. Form FS-1. North Carolina's certification of liability insurance (NCDMV insurance requirements), used to register the car and issue or transfer your plate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is full coverage required by North Carolina law or by my auto lender?

By your lender. North Carolina law requires liability coverage of at least fifty thousand dollars per person and one hundred thousand dollars per accident for bodily injury and fifty thousand dollars for property damage, plus uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage. State law does not require Comprehensive or Collision. Your loan or lease contract does, because the lender or leasing company has a financial interest in the car until it is paid off or returned.

What maximum Comprehensive and Collision deductible do most NC auto lenders allow?

Each lender sets its own maximum in the loan agreement. Many cap the deductible at one thousand dollars, and some require five hundred dollars or less, especially on leases. Check your contract or ask the dealership's finance office before you choose a deductible, and tell our team the limit so every quote we compare fits it.

Why do North Carolina car leases often require higher liability limits than state minimums?

The leasing company still owns the car, so it wants more protection than the state minimum provides. Many lease agreements require liability limits well above fifty, one hundred, fifty, and they spell out the exact limits in the contract. Bring the insurance section of your lease to our team and we will quote limits that meet it.

What happens if I drop Comprehensive or Collision while I still owe money on my car loan?

Dropping required coverage usually breaks the terms of your loan. Your lender is notified and may buy collateral protection insurance and add the cost to your loan. That coverage protects the lender's interest, not yours, and it is often more expensive than a policy you choose. Keep Comprehensive and Collision until the loan is paid off and the lien is released.

How fast can an independent NC agent send Form FS-1 and a lienholder binder to my dealership?

Often the same business day. Once you choose a carrier and the policy is bound, our team can send the Form FS-1 for registration and an insurance binder showing your lender as lienholder or loss payee directly to the dealership. Timing depends on the carrier and how quickly we receive the vehicle and lender details, so call or text us before you head to the dealership.
