The North Carolina Rate Bureau sets homeowners base rates by territory, and the territories follow county lines. That is why two Chapel Hill homes a mile apart can start from different base rates. Here is how the two Triangle territories compare, using the Department of Insurance's published data.

Same Triangle, two rating territories: the county line decides which one is yours.

The county-line catch. Chapel Hill crosses into Durham County on its east side, and parts of it sit in Chatham County to the south. The 27517 ZIP code crosses county lines too. A Chapel Hill home in Durham County rates in Territory 270, while one in Orange or Chatham County rates in Territory 280. The same goes for Cary, whose Chatham County neighborhoods are in Territory 280, and Mebane, whose Alamance County side is in a different territory altogether (310).

How do big trees, wind and hail deductibles and older plumbing affect Triangle homes?

Neighborhoods like historic Chapel Hill, Carrboro, Watts-Hillandale, Trinity Park and inside-the-Beltline Raleigh have mature hardwoods and older homes. That shapes coverage in a few ways:

  • Fallen trees. A tree that falls on your house, garage or fence in a storm is generally covered under the dwelling or other structures coverage. Removing a tree that hits nothing is often limited or excluded. Ask what your policy pays for debris removal.
  • Wind and hail deductibles. Some policies use a flat deductible, for example $1,000 or $2,500. Others use a percentage of your dwelling limit, for example 1 or 2 percent, which can be much larger on a big claim. Check which one your quote uses.
  • Water backup. Backups through drains or sump pumps are usually excluded unless you add water backup coverage. That matters in older homes with 1950s to 1990s plumbing.
  • Service lines. Service line coverage helps with buried water, sewer and electric lines on your property, which are common trouble spots in older neighborhoods.

Mature oaks are part of the Triangle's charm, and part of your coverage plan.

What do condo, townhome, renters and landlord owners need to know?

  • Condos and townhomes (HO-6). In communities like Meadowmont, Southern Village, Briar Chapel, downtown Durham and Cary, the association's master policy may be "bare walls-in" (it covers the structure, and you cover the interior) or "all-in" (it covers fixtures and finishes too). Your HO-6 policy fills the gap, and loss assessment coverage helps if the association bills owners after a big claim.
  • Renters (HO-4). Grad students, residents and faculty renting along NC-54, in Carrboro or near Ninth Street need their own renters policy. Your landlord's policy does not cover your belongings or your liability. Start a renters quote.
  • Landlords (DP-3). Rental homes need a dwelling policy, not a homeowners policy. Under a settlement announced by the Department of Insurance, dwelling base rates rise an average of 5 percent statewide on October 1, 2026, and another 5 percent on October 1, 2027, varying by territory (NCDOI announcement).

Renting in Chapel Hill, Carrboro, Durham or Raleigh? Most leases now require renters insurance with the landlord listed as an interested party — we email proof once your policy is bound.

Are NC minimum auto limits enough for an I-40 or RTP commute?

Since July 1, 2025, North Carolina requires at least 50/100/50 liability limits under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-279.21. That is a legal floor. A multi-car crash on I-40, US-15-501, NC-54, NC-147 or I-540 involving a newer EV or luxury SUV can run past those limits quickly, and you could be responsible for the difference.

  • Higher liability. Many commuters choose limits such as 100/300/100 or higher, and some add a personal umbrella.
  • UM/UIM. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage protects you when the other driver has too little insurance. Carriers offer higher combined limits, up to one million dollars where available.
  • Medical payments. Helps with medical bills for you and your passengers regardless of fault.
  • New car replacement. Some carriers offer coverage that replaces a new vehicle rather than paying its depreciated value after a total loss.
  • SDIP points. North Carolina's Safe Driver Incentive Plan (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-65) adds surcharges for at-fault accidents and moving violations, generally for three policy years.
  • College students. Most carriers expect every licensed driver in the household to be listed, including a student at UNC, Duke or NC State. Some offer a discount when the student is at school far from home without a car. Tell us before you remove anyone.

Triangle commutes are busy, and minimum limits can run out fast.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does a 27517 Chapel Hill address rate differently depending on Orange, Durham, or Chatham County?

North Carolina homeowners rating territories follow county lines, not town names or ZIP codes. Orange and Chatham counties are Territory 280, while Durham and Wake counties are Territory 270. The 27517 ZIP code crosses county lines, so two Chapel Hill addresses in the same ZIP can land in different territories with different base rates. We check the county on your parcel record before we quote, so your home is rated in the right territory.

Do UNC, Duke, or NC State students living off-campus need separate renters or auto updates?

Often, yes. A student in an off-campus apartment may need a renters policy, because a parent's homeowners coverage for belongings away from home is usually limited. On the auto side, most carriers expect every licensed driver in the household to be listed, including students away at school, and some offer a discount for a student who attends school far from home without a car. Call or text our team to review your situation.

Do Triangle condo and townhome owners need an HO-6 policy?

Most do. Your homeowners association's master policy may cover only the bare walls, or it may include fixtures and finishes inside the unit, depending on the bylaws. An HO-6 condo policy covers what the master policy does not, plus your belongings and liability, and loss assessment coverage helps if the association bills owners after a large claim. Send us your association's insurance summary and we will match the coverage to it.

Are North Carolina landlord dwelling insurance rates going up in 2026?

Yes. Under a settlement announced by the North Carolina Department of Insurance, dwelling base rates rise an average of five percent statewide on October first, two thousand twenty-six, and another five percent on October first, two thousand twenty-seven. The change varies by territory. Dwelling policies cover rental and investment homes, so Triangle landlords with rental properties should review their renewals.
