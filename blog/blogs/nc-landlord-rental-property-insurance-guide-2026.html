North Carolina Landlord & Rental Property Insurance Guide (2026): DP-1 vs. DP-3, Loss of Rents & FAIR Plan Options

What changes the day a tenant moves in, which dwelling form actually protects your rental, and how to read your October 2026 renewal.

By Bill Layne, licensed NC agent since 2005Updated October 26, 202610 min read

The 40-second takeaway

A standard North Carolina HO-3 homeowners policy is written for a home you live in. Once you move out and rent the house to long-term tenants, keeping that HO-3 in place can lead to a denied claim for material misrepresentation, because the policy’s occupancy condition is no longer true.

NC landlords need a Dwelling Fire or Landlord policy. For most single-family rentals, the right setup is a DP-3 Special Form (open-peril, replacement cost on the building), plus Coverage D: Fair Rental Value (loss of rents) and Coverage L: Premises Liability.

Why Your Regular NC Homeowners (HO-3) Policy Fails When Tenants Move In

Every HO-3 policy defines a “residence premises”: the dwelling where you live, shown on your declarations page. Coverage for the house, your belongings and your liability is built on that definition. When you stop living there and a tenant does, the house no longer fits the policy’s definition, even though the declarations page still lists the address.

Material misrepresentation
A fact the insurer relied on to issue or price the policy that is no longer accurate. Occupancy is one of the biggest. If it changed and the company was never told, a claim can be denied or the policy rescinded.

Where NC homeowners get caught

  • Keeping the starter home. You buy a new house and keep your first place in Elkin, Mount Airy or Winston-Salem as a long-term rental, but never change the policy.
  • Inheriting a family home. A parent’s house in Surry, Wilkes or Yadkin County passes to you. It sits vacant during probate or renovation, then gets rented. Vacancy and tenant occupancy both need to be disclosed; many policies limit coverage after a set number of days vacant.
  • Short-term rentals. A mountain cabin or Yadkin Valley cottage listed on Airbnb or VRBO without a short-term rental endorsement or a specialty landlord policy built for nightly guests.

Escrow is not approval. If your mortgage company keeps paying the premium from escrow, that only means the bill is paid. It does not mean the insurer knows or approved the occupancy change. Tell us before the tenant moves in so the right policy is in place on day one.

DP-1 vs. DP-2 vs. DP-3 Dwelling Fire Policies in North Carolina

A dwelling fire policy NC landlords buy comes in three standard forms. The difference is how many causes of loss are covered and how a claim is paid.

DP-1 Basic Form

Named-peril only: fire, lightning and internal explosion. Windstorm, hail and vandalism usually require an Extended Coverage or vandalism endorsement. Claims on the structure and roof are settled at Actual Cash Value (after depreciation), and sudden accidental discharge of water from a burst pipe is not covered.

DP-2 Broad Form

Adds more named perils, including weight of ice and snow, accidental discharge of water or steam, and falling objects. It typically offers Replacement Cost on the dwelling when you insure to at least 80% of rebuild value.

DP-3 Special Form: the standard for NC landlords

Open-peril (“all-risk”) coverage on Coverage A (Dwelling) and Coverage B (Other Structures). Any sudden, accidental direct physical loss is covered unless the policy specifically excludes it, and the building is settled at Replacement Cost. For most tenant-occupied single-family homes, DP-1 vs DP-3 North Carolina comes down to this: DP-3 covers far more, and pays to rebuild instead of paying a depreciated amount.

Coverage D (Loss of Rents) & Coverage L (Premises Liability)

Coverage D: Fair Rental Value

Loss of rents coverage NC landlords carry reimburses the rent you lose when a covered loss, like a kitchen fire, a fallen oak or a windstorm, makes the rental uninhabitable during repairs. Your mortgage, taxes and insurance bills don’t stop when the tenant has to move out. Coverage D keeps that income coming.

Check two numbers on your declarations page:

  • The dollar limit. Often set at 10% of Coverage A. On a home insured for 250,000, that’s 25,000. If your house rents for 1,800 a month, that covers about 14 months.
  • The time limit. Some policies cap Coverage D at 12 months; others allow 24. After a major fire, permits and contractor backlogs in the foothills can push repairs past a year.

Coverage L & M: Premises Liability and Medical Payments

The NC Department of Insurance notes that basic dwelling fire forms do not automatically include liability coverage. It must be added by endorsement or packaged into a landlord policy. Without it, a tenant’s guest who falls on a rotten step leaves you defending the claim yourself.

We generally recommend 300,000 to 500,000 in premises liability per property. If you own several rentals or hold them in an LLC, add a personal umbrella or a commercial liability policy for the portfolio.

Coverage C on a landlord policy

Coverage C on a rental covers only what you own and leave on site: refrigerator, stove, washer and dryer, a lawnmower in the shed. It does not cover the tenant’s furniture, clothes or electronics.

Every NC lease should require the tenant to carry an HO-4 renters insurance policy with 100,000 to 300,000 in tenant liability, naming you or your property manager as an Interested Party so you’re notified if it lapses.

How the October 2026 NC Dwelling Rate Settlement Affects Rental Renewals

On October 30, 2025, the North Carolina Rate Bureau, which represents insurance companies and is separate from the Department of Insurance, requested a 68.3% statewide average increase on dwelling policies over two years. On April 22, 2026, Commissioner Mike Causey announced a negotiated settlement instead:

  • 5.0% average increase effective October 1, 2026
  • 5.0% average increase effective October 1, 2027

The settlement also includes mitigation credits for fortified homes and roofs. For more detail, see our October 2026 rental rate change breakdown.

Why your renewal can still go up more than 5%

The 5% figures are statewide averages, and they vary by territory. Your own bill can rise more because of:

  • Inflation guard. Coverage A increases automatically each year to track rebuild costs, which raises premium on top of the rate change.
  • Territory. Coastal, Piedmont and Foothills territories carry different rates.
  • Deductible changes. A shift in your wind/hail or all-peril deductible changes the price.
  • Consent to rate. Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 58-36-30, an insurer can charge above the Rate Bureau rate with your signed consent to rate letter.

5-point renewal declarations page audit

  1. Form type. Confirm it says DP-3 (or the carrier’s landlord special form), not DP-1, unless you chose basic coverage on purpose.
  2. Coverage A limit. Compare it to current rebuild cost, not market value or purchase price.
  3. Coverage D limit and time cap. Divide the limit by your monthly rent. Aim for at least 12 months.
  4. Liability. Make sure Coverage L is listed, at the limit you want, for this address.
  5. Deductibles and consent to rate. Check the all-peril and wind/hail deductibles and look for any consent to rate form.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I keep my regular NC homeowners policy when I rent out my house?

No. A standard HO-3 homeowners policy requires that you live in the home. Once long-term tenants move in, the occupancy condition is no longer true and a claim can be denied for material misrepresentation. You need a dwelling fire or landlord policy, usually a DP-3, before the lease starts.

What is the difference between a DP-1 and a DP-3 landlord policy in North Carolina?

A DP-1 covers only named perils such as fire, lightning and internal explosion, pays claims at actual cash value after depreciation, and does not cover sudden water discharge from a burst pipe. A DP-3 covers any sudden accidental loss not specifically excluded and pays to rebuild the structure at replacement cost.

How does Loss of Rents (Fair Rental Value) coverage work on an NC rental policy?

Coverage D reimburses the rent you lose when a covered loss makes the rental unlivable during repairs. Check both the dollar limit, often ten percent of the dwelling limit, and the time limit, commonly twelve or twenty-four months, against your actual monthly rent.

Does North Carolina landlord insurance cover my tenant's personal belongings?

No. Landlord personal property coverage only protects items you own and leave at the rental, such as appliances and maintenance equipment. Tenants need their own HO-4 renters policy, and your lease should require it with you listed as an interested party.

Which insurance companies write rental properties and older landlord homes in North Carolina?

Our agency compares Steadily, NC Grange Mutual, Alamance Farmers Mutual, Travelers, National General, Universal Property, Progressive, Nationwide and Foremost. For older roofs or properties standard carriers decline, the NCJUA FAIR Plan is the market of last resort.
