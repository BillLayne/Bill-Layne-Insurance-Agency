Dropped or Non-Renewed by Your Home Insurance in NC? How the NCJUA FAIR Plan Works (2026)

Why NC carriers drop older homes and rentals, what to do before your coverage ends, and how the state’s FAIR Plan actually works.

By Bill Layne, licensed NC agent since 2005Published October 22, 202610 min read

Quick answer

If your North Carolina home insurance company non-renews you, you’ll usually get at least 45 days’ written notice. Use that time to have an independent agent check regional carriers first. If no standard company will write the property, the NCJUA FAIR Plan is an industry-run market of last resort that offers basic dwelling coverage—still with underwriting, photos and limits.

The letter usually shows up with no warning. You’ve paid on time for years, never filed a claim, and now your insurance company says it won’t renew. The reason line says something like “roof age,” “exterior condition” or “property no longer meets underwriting guidelines.”

I see these every month in Surry, Wilkes and Yadkin counties. Getting dropped by your home insurance in NC feels like a verdict on your house. Most of the time it’s really a verdict on one company’s rulebook. Here’s how to work through it.

Why Do NC Home Insurance Companies Non-Renew Properties?

A non-renewal isn’t a cancellation. The company is honoring the policy through its end date, then declining to write another term. Under North Carolina law it has to tell you why. The most common reasons I see:

  • Roof age or condition. Many carriers tighten up once a shingle roof passes a certain age, or when an inspection photo shows curling, missing shingles or moss.
  • Exterior condition. Weathered or rotted siding, peeling paint, damaged gutters, loose railings and porch steps all show up in drive-by and aerial inspections.
  • Older manufactured homes. Single-wides and pre-1976 homes fall outside a lot of standard programs. Our NC mobile and manufactured home insurance guide covers what specialty carriers look for.
  • Rental or vacant occupancy. A homeowners policy is written for the person living there. Once the home becomes a rental, sits empty or turns into an inherited property, it often needs a dwelling policy instead.
  • Claims history or the carrier’s own book. Sometimes it’s a prior claim. Sometimes the company is simply reducing how many homes it writes in an area.

What Should You Do in Your Notice Window Before Coverage Expires?

North Carolina generally requires insurers to mail a non-renewal notice at least 45 days before the policy ends (G.S. 58-41-20). If you have a mortgage, your lender gets a copy too. Don’t let that time drift. A gap in coverage can trigger lender-placed insurance, which typically costs more and protects the lender, not you.

Your non-renewal game plan

Work through these in order, starting the week the letter arrives.

  • Read the stated reason. Is it something you can fix, like a roof, railing or paint?
  • Get repair estimates. A roof replacement or exterior repair can reopen standard markets.
  • Gather photos of all four sides, the roof, and any recent updates with dates.
  • Have an independent agent shop regional and standard carriers before anything else.
  • Only then, apply to the NCJUA FAIR Plan if the standard market says no.
  • Send your lender the new declarations page before the old policy expires.

Check regional carriers first

Big national carriers often run the strictest roof-age and condition rules. Regional companies tend to look at the actual house. For the homes we see around Elkin and the Yadkin Valley, the first calls usually go to:

  • NC Grange Mutual. A North Carolina mutual with deep rural property roots.
  • Alamance Farmers Mutual. A North Carolina farmers mutual that understands older country homes and outbuildings.
  • Universal Property. Another option we check for homes that don’t fit the national carriers’ rules.

Each has its own guidelines, so a decline from one is not a decline from all of them. That’s the core reason to work with an independent agent instead of buying online.

How Does the NCJUA FAIR Plan Work in North Carolina?

NCJUA (North Carolina FAIR Plan)
The North Carolina Joint Underwriting Association, also called the FAIR (Fair Access to Insurance Requirements) Plan. It’s an association of the insurance companies licensed to write property insurance in North Carolina, created by law to offer basic property coverage when the standard market won’t.

Three things people get wrong about it:

It’s not a government giveaway

The NCJUA isn’t a state agency and isn’t funded by taxpayers. It’s run by the insurance industry under oversight from the NC Department of Insurance. Premiums aren’t subsidized, and pricing isn’t necessarily lower than the standard market.

It still underwrites your home

The FAIR Plan asks for photos of the dwelling and can require repairs, exclude outbuildings in poor shape, or decline a property. It’s built for insurable homes that private carriers won’t take—not homes with unrepaired damage.

It covers most of the state, but not the beach

The NCJUA writes property across North Carolina except the designated beach area, which is handled by its coastal counterpart, the NCIUA. For Surry, Wilkes, Yadkin, Alleghany and Stokes counties, the NCJUA is the relevant plan. You can read the plan’s own materials at ncjua-nciua.org.

Treat it as a bridge. Many owners place a home with the FAIR Plan, fix the roof or siding, then move back to a standard carrier. Ask your agent to re-shop the property every renewal.

DP-1 vs. DP-2: What’s the Difference Between Basic and Broad Dwelling Fire Policies?

FAIR Plan coverage, and most landlord coverage, is written on a dwelling fire form instead of a homeowners form. The two you’ll hear most:

FormWhat it coversTypical fit
DP-1 (Basic)A short list of named perils: fire, lightning and internal explosion, with windstorm, hail and similar perils often added as extended coverageOlder or lower-value homes, vacant property, tight budgets
DP-2 (Broad)Everything in basic, plus more named perils such as falling objects, weight of ice and snow, and certain sudden water and freezing lossesOwner-occupied homes and rentals where you want wider protection

Policy form is not the same as settlement method

This trips up a lot of people. The form (DP-1 or DP-2) decides which causes of loss are covered. The settlement method decides how much you get paid for a covered loss.

Replacement Cost
Pays to repair or replace with like kind and quality, without subtracting depreciation, up to your limit. Usually requires insuring the home close to its full rebuild cost.
Actual Cash Value (ACV)
Pays replacement cost minus depreciation for age and wear. On an older roof or siding, that deduction can be large.

You can have a broad form that pays ACV, or a basic form that pays replacement cost, depending on the property and the carrier. Always confirm both on the quote.

What Separate Coverages Do You Need to Ask About?

A homeowners policy bundles a lot together. A dwelling fire or FAIR Plan policy usually doesn’t. Before you sign, ask about each of these:

  • Premises liability. Dwelling policies, including NCJUA policies, generally don’t include liability. If a tenant, guest or delivery driver gets hurt on the property, you need a separate liability policy or endorsement. Landlords should treat this as non-negotiable.
  • Flood. No dwelling or homeowners policy covers flood from rising water. Homes near the Yadkin River, Big Elkin Creek or any low-lying creek bottom should price a separate flood policy.
  • Contents. Personal property is optional on a dwelling policy. Owner-occupants should add it. Landlords may need it for appliances and furnishings they supply.
  • Loss of rents or fair rental value. If a covered loss makes a rental unlivable, this replaces the rent you lose during repairs.

Frequently Asked Questions About Non-Renewals and the NCJUA

How much notice does an NC insurance company have to give before non-renewing my home policy?

North Carolina generally requires at least forty-five days of written notice before a homeowners or dwelling policy is non-renewed, and the notice must state the reason. Your lender or mortgagee on the policy gets a copy too. Start shopping the day the letter arrives.

Is the NCJUA FAIR Plan government insurance?

No. The North Carolina Joint Underwriting Association, also called the FAIR Plan, is an association of insurance companies licensed to write property insurance in North Carolina. It was created by law as a market of last resort and is overseen by the NC Commissioner of Insurance, but it is not a state agency.

Will the NCJUA insure any house that gets turned down?

Not automatically. The NCJUA has its own underwriting rules, asks for photos of the property, and can require repairs or decline a home in poor condition. It is designed for insurable property that the standard market will not take, not for property with unrepaired damage.

Does an NCJUA dwelling policy include liability coverage?

Generally no. NCJUA dwelling policies focus on the building and, if you choose it, your belongings. If you need liability protection for guests, tenants or visitors, ask your agent about a separate premises liability policy.

Can I move off the FAIR Plan later?

Yes. Many owners use the NCJUA as a bridge. After a roof replacement, exterior repairs or a clean claim history, it is worth having your agent re-shop the property with standard carriers at each renewal.
