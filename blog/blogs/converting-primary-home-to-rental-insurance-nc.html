Moving out and keeping your house as a rental? Here’s why your homeowners policy needs to change before your tenants move in.

Why does your HO-3 stop fitting when you rent out your house?

A standard HO-3 homeowners policy is written for the home you live in. Its coverages assume you’re the one living there: your belongings, your family’s liability, your extra living costs if the house is damaged. Once you move out and tenants move in, that picture changes, and the old policy can leave property and liability claims uncovered or in dispute.

The fix is simple: switch to a landlord dwelling policy effective on or before the day your tenants take possession.

What is a North Carolina dwelling (landlord) policy?

The North Carolina Department of Insurance explains the difference this way:

“The coverage is very similar to a homeowners policy, with one significant difference – a dwelling fire policy is typically used for someone that does not make the property their primary residence. Dwelling policies typically do not provide liability coverage, like some homeowners policy forms, but they can provide other similar coverages to standard homeowners policies.”— NCDOI: Dwelling Policies

And on the most complete form:

“The DP-3 form is the most comprehensive dwelling fire coverage available. It is an ‘open perils’ or ‘all risk’ policy, which means real property (dwelling and other structures) will be covered for all types of damage, except exclusions named in the policy.”— NCDOI: Dwelling Policies

Two things to note. First, the DP-3 covers your own personal property in the rental (like appliances you leave behind) on a named-perils basis, not open perils. Second, because dwelling forms don’t automatically include liability, landlords usually add premises liability by endorsement.

Dwelling policies are also rated separately from homeowners policies in North Carolina. Under a settlement announced by the Department of Insurance in April 2026, dwelling base rates rose an average of 5 percent statewide on October 1, 2026, with another 5 percent average increase scheduled for October 1, 2027, varying by territory (NCDOI announcement).

The HO-3 stops fitting when you move. The DP-3 is built for tenants.

Can you require tenants to carry renters insurance?

Yes. Your dwelling policy doesn’t cover your tenant’s belongings, so many landlords require renters (HO-4) insurance in the lease. North Carolina law sets one clear rule:

“The tenant shall not be required to obtain the required insurance coverage from a designated carrier or through a designated agent.”— N.C.G.S. § 42-46(l)(1)

Subsection (l)(2) also lets a landlord charge certain limited costs if the tenant doesn’t provide proof of coverage within three business days of the landlord’s request. Have your lease reviewed before relying on that provision.

Four things to line up before your tenants move in.

Your move-out switch checklist

  1. Pin down the lease start date. That’s the date the new landlord policy needs to be in force.
  2. Choose the form. A DP-3 for open-perils protection on the structure, or a DP-1 if you only want basic named perils.
  3. Add premises liability. It isn’t automatic on dwelling forms.
  4. Add Fair Rental Value. It replaces lost rent if a covered loss makes the house unlivable during repairs.
  5. Update your mortgage servicer. Send the new declarations page with the mortgagee clause effective on move-out day, and confirm escrow will pay the new policy.
  6. Require tenant renters insurance in the lease, letting the tenant choose their own carrier.
  7. Cancel the old HO-3 effective the same day the landlord policy starts, so there’s no gap or overlap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my homeowners policy cover my house after I move out and rent it to tenants?

Usually not the way you need it to. A standard HO-3 homeowners policy is written for a home you live in. Once tenants move in and you move out, the policy may not respond to property or liability claims the way you expect. Before your tenants' move-in date, talk with our team about switching to a landlord dwelling policy.

What is the difference between a DP-1 and a DP-3 landlord policy in North Carolina?

A DP-1 is a basic named perils policy that covers a short list of causes such as fire, lightning and internal explosion, and it usually pays actual cash value unless you add replacement cost. A DP-3 is the most comprehensive dwelling form. It covers the house and other structures on an open perils basis, meaning all damage except what the policy excludes.

Does a North Carolina dwelling policy include landlord liability coverage?

Not automatically. The North Carolina Department of Insurance notes that dwelling policies typically do not provide liability coverage the way some homeowners forms do. Landlords usually add premises liability by endorsement or a separate policy, so ask our team to include it when we quote your rental.

Can I require my tenants to carry renters insurance in North Carolina?

Yes, your lease can require it. North Carolina law says the tenant cannot be required to buy that coverage from a specific insurance company or through a specific agent. If the tenant does not show proof of coverage within three business days after you ask, the law lets the landlord charge certain limited costs. Read the statute or talk with an attorney before writing that into your lease.

What should I tell my mortgage company when I turn my house into a rental?

Tell your mortgage servicer that the home is becoming a rental and send them the new landlord policy's declarations page showing them as mortgagee, effective on your move-out date. If your premium is paid through escrow, confirm the servicer will pay the new policy and stop paying the old one. Our team can send the mortgagee information directly once the new policy is bound.
