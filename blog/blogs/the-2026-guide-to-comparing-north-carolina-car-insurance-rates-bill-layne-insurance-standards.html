What are the 2026 North Carolina car insurance requirements?

As of July 1, 2025, North Carolina law mandates minimum liability coverage limits of $50,000 for bodily injury per person, $100,000 per accident, and $50,000 for property damage (Classed as 50/100/50).

If you are still holding a policy structured around the old "30/60/25" metrics, you are non-compliant and underinsured. The state increased these limits because medical bills and vehicle prices have outpaced the old standards. Below is a comparison of how the "Bill Layne Standard" aligns with state laws.

How do severe weather events impact NC insurance policies?

Severe weather events in NC impact policies through the introduction of Named Storm Deductibles (1% to 10% of insured value) and strict 30-day waiting periods for new flood insurance applications.

Living in Elkin and Surry County, we are somewhat inland, but the devastation of Hurricane Helene proved that wind and flood risks are not just coastal problems.

The Difference Between Water and Wind

  • Flood Insurance: Standard home and auto policies do not cover flood damage (rising water). You must purchase a separate policy. Crucial Note: There is a strict 30-day waiting period before a flood policy becomes active. Do not wait for the forecast to buy it.
  • Wind & Hail (NCIUA): In some high-risk areas, traditional carriers may exclude wind. We access the NCIUA (North Carolina Insurance Underwriting Association), also known as the "Coastal Property Insurance Pool," to ensure your home is protected from windstorms, even if major carriers pull back capacity.
