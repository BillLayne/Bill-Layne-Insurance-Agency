NC Mobile Home Insurance: Park, Lot & Lender Rules

Your park lease or loan papers decide what your policy has to show. Here’s how to read them and get the right proof in place.

Quick answer

North Carolina law does not mandate insurance on an owned manufactured home paid in full on private land, so your park lease or loan contract sets the requirement. Mobile home parks typically require personal liability naming the park as an interested party, while chattel lenders require physical-damage coverage listing them as loss payee.

The lease and the loan papers tell you exactly what your policy has to show.

This guide is for manufactured home buyers and owners across inland North Carolina, including the Piedmont, the Sandhills and Fayetteville, the Triad, Charlotte, the Yadkin Valley and Surry County, and Western NC. It covers the paperwork side: what your park or lender wants to see on the policy. Who sets the insurance requirement on a mobile home in NC?

North Carolina doesn’t set a statutory insurance minimum for a manufactured home you own outright on private land. The requirement almost always comes from a document you signed:

  • Your park lease if the home sits on a rented lot.
  • Your loan agreement if the home is financed.

Those documents set the coverage you must carry, who must be listed on the policy, and the highest deductible allowed. If you have both a lease and a loan, you need to meet both.

What are the MH(C) and MH(F) forms, and how do they settle claims?

The North Carolina Department of Insurance describes the policies this way:

“Mobile homeowners insurance policies available in North Carolina may be used to provide coverage for your manufactured home, personal property and personal liability. The two forms of mobile homeowners policies offered in North Carolina are the MH (C) and MH (F) form.”— NCDOI: Manufactured Homes

The settlement basis matters to many lenders:

“Physical damage losses are settled on an actual cash value basis (i.e. replacement cost less depreciation), unless replacement cost coverage is purchased.”— NCDOI: Manufactured Homes

Some lenders accept actual cash value. Others ask for replacement cost or a specific deductible. Check your loan instructions before you compare quotes, so every quote meets them. The Department’s manufactured homes guidance walks through property and liability coverages under both forms.

Check the settlement type before you compare prices.

Title, loss payee or mortgagee: which one applies to you?

How your lender appears on the policy depends on whether the home still has a DMV title. North Carolina’s title law explains when the title goes away:

“If a certificate of title has been issued for a manufactured home, the owner listed on the title has the title, and the manufactured home qualifies as real property as defined in G.S. 105-273(13), the owner listed on the title shall submit an affidavit to the Division that the manufactured home meets this definition and surrender the certificate of title to the Division.”— N.C.G.S. § 20-109.2(a)
  • Home on a rented lot. It keeps its NCDMV title. A chattel lender is listed as loss payee.
  • Home affixed to land you own, title surrendered. Once the home meets the real-property definition in N.C.G.S. § 105-273(13) and the DMV title is surrendered, the lender is listed in a real-property mortgagee clause, and the first year’s premium is often paid through closing escrow.

How do you get the right proof to your lender or park?

  1. Gather your instructions. The insurance section of your park lease, your lender’s insurance instruction sheet, and any closing attorney contact.
  2. Note the exact wording. The lender’s name and address for the loss payee or mortgagee clause (including ISAOA/ATIMA if requested), and the park’s name for the interested-party listing.
  3. Check the limits. The maximum deductible, any minimum liability limit, and whether replacement cost is required.
  4. Compare and bind. We compare our appointed manufactured home carriers, including Foremost. Each has its own rules.
  5. Send the proof. Once the policy is bound, the binder or declarations page goes to your lender, park or closing attorney, along with an escrow invoice if your closing needs one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does North Carolina law require insurance on a mobile home?

Not for a home you own outright on private land. The requirement usually comes from paperwork you signed: a park lease or a loan agreement. Those documents set the coverage you must carry, who must be listed on the policy and the highest deductible allowed. Read both, or send them to our team and we will match a policy to them.

What is the difference between a loss payee and a mortgagee on a manufactured home policy?

A chattel lender, whose loan is secured by a home that keeps its North Carolina DMV title, is usually listed as loss payee. A lender on a land and home mortgage, where the title was surrendered and the home is real property, is usually listed in a mortgagee clause. Either way, use the exact name and address your lender gives you so claim checks and notices reach them.

What does a mobile home park usually require from residents?

Many park leases require personal liability coverage and ask that the park be listed on your policy as an interested party or additional interest, so the park is notified if coverage ends. The lease spells out the exact wording and any minimum limit. Bring the insurance section of your lease to our team before you move in.

Does my lender care whether my policy pays actual cash value or replacement cost?

It can. North Carolina mobile homeowners policies settle physical damage at actual cash value, which subtracts depreciation, unless replacement cost coverage is purchased. Some lenders accept either, and others ask for a specific settlement basis or a maximum deductible. Check your loan instructions and ask our team which basis your quote uses.

Does requesting a quote satisfy my lender or park?

No. A quote only shows the price and coverage being offered. Your lender or park needs proof of an active policy, usually a binder or declarations page listing them correctly. Coverage is in place only after the policy is bound and our team confirms it, so start early before your closing or move-in date.
