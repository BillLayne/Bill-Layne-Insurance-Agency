How to Transfer an Out-of-State Driver’s License to North Carolina (2026): DL-123, FS-1 & the 3-Step Order

What you need to get your license in NC from out of state, in the order the DMV actually requires it.

By Bill Layne, Licensed NC Insurance Agent (Agency License #15569731), serving all 100 NC counties since 2005Updated October 28, 20268 min read

The 40-second answer

Under North Carolina law (N.C.G.S. 20-7 and 20-309), new residents have 60 days to transfer an out-of-state driver’s license and vehicle registration to NC. The catch: the DMV won’t issue either one until you have a North Carolina auto policy. Do it in this order:

  1. Get NC insurance first. Switch to a policy from a North Carolina-licensed insurance company (NCDMV won’t accept an out-of-state policy). Your agent provides Form DL-123 (for your license) and Form FS-1 (for your plate).
  2. Visit an NC DMV Driver License Office with your DL-123, out-of-state license, proof of SSN and 2 proofs of NC residency to get your NC license (or temporary driving certificate).
  3. Visit an NC License Plate Agency (a separate office) with your new NC license, out-of-state title or lienholder info, FS-1 and title and Highway Use Tax fees to get your plate and registration.

Do You Need Insurance to Get Your License in NC? Why Out-of-State Policies Get Turned Away

Every week, new residents wait in line at the Elkin, Mount Airy, Wilkesboro or Charlotte DMV, reach the window with a Florida, Virginia, New York or California insurance card, and get sent home. North Carolina doesn’t accept it, even if that policy is fully paid and valid where it was written.

NC requires continuous liability coverage on a North Carolina policy form that meets the state’s minimum limits:

NC minimum liability (50/100/50)
$50,000 bodily injury per person, $100,000 bodily injury per accident and $50,000 property damage, plus Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage.

DL-123 Insurance Form vs. FS-1 Form NC: Why New Residents Need Both

The DL-123 is provided by a North Carolina insurance company and certifies you as a driver. The FS-1 certifies a specific vehicle. You need both because the license and the plate are issued at two different offices.

What Do I Need to Bring to Get My License in NC? (2026 Checklist)

For an NC out-of-state license transfer, bring these to the Driver License Office:

  • Your out-of-state driver’s license. A valid license (or one expired less than 2 years) usually waives the road and written tests.
  • Form DL-123 from a North Carolina-licensed insurance agent, dated within 30 days.
  • One proof of age and identity: U.S. passport, certified birth certificate or a valid REAL ID document.
  • Proof of Social Security number: SSN card, W-2 or 1099.
  • Two proofs of NC physical address (for REAL ID): NC closing disclosure or deed, signed NC lease, utility bill, or your new NC auto or homeowners declarations page.

Pro tip: In North Carolina, Driver License Offices and License Plate Agencies are two separate buildings run separately. Get your license first, then drive to the plate agency.

How to Transfer Your Out-of-State Title and License Plate (Within 60 Days)

At the NC License Plate Agency, bring:

  • Your new NC driver’s license (or temporary driving certificate).
  • Your out-of-state Certificate of Title. If the car is financed or leased and your lender holds the title, bring your current out-of-state registration card plus the lienholder’s name, address and your account number. NCDMV requests the title from the lender.
  • An active Form FS-1 matching your 17-digit VIN.
  • A notarized Title Application (Form MVR-1). Most plate agencies have a notary at the counter.
  • Payment for the title fee, registration fee and Highway Use Tax.

Do I need an NC inspection first?

Generally no. A vehicle registered in NC for the first time typically isn’t inspected until its first registration renewal. Confirm with your plate agency if your vehicle has special status.

Your old state’s plates: Some states, including New York, New Jersey and Pennsylvania, require you to surrender or mail back old plates before you cancel that state’s registration and insurance. Check your prior state’s rules so you don’t get a lapse notice after you move.

The Hidden “Inexperienced Driver” Surcharge for Out-of-State Movers (and How We Fix It)

North Carolina’s Safe Driver Incentive Plan (SDIP) adds a surcharge for inexperienced operators. Drivers first licensed on or after July 1, 2025 can be rated as inexperienced for up to 8 years; drivers licensed earlier fall under the prior 3-year rule. Frequently Asked Questions

Do you need North Carolina insurance to transfer an out-of-state driver's license to NC?

Yes. North Carolina requires proof of active liability insurance issued by a company licensed to do business in North Carolina on Form DL-123 before issuing an NC driver's license. Out-of-state auto policies are not accepted by the NCDMV.

How long do I have to transfer my driver's license and license plate after moving to North Carolina?

New North Carolina residents have 60 days from establishing residency to obtain a North Carolina driver's license and register their vehicle at an NC License Plate Agency.

What is the difference between an NC DL-123 form and an FS-1 form?

Form DL-123 proves that you as an individual driver carry at least 50,000 dollars per person, 100,000 dollars per accident in bodily injury, and 50,000 dollars in property damage liability for the Driver License Office. Form FS-1 proves that a specific vehicle VIN is insured so the License Plate Agency can issue your metal tag.

Can I register my car in North Carolina if my bank or lienholder still has the out-of-state title?

Yes. Bring your current out-of-state registration card, your new NC driver's license, your active NC Form FS-1, and your lender's name and mailing address to the NC License Plate Agency.

How do I avoid the North Carolina inexperienced driver surcharge when moving from another state?

Provide your original out-of-state driver's license or a certified driving history abstract from your previous state's DMV so your independent agent can verify your prior years of driving experience.
