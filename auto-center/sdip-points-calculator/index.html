Understanding NC Driving Records

Why DMV Points & Insurance Points Differ

North Carolina uses two separate point systems. Knowing which one applies to your ticket or accident helps you avoid costly surprises at renewal.

NC DMV License Points

Tracks your driving privilege

Managed by the NC Division of Motor Vehicles to monitor traffic safety on North Carolina roads.

  • 12 points in 3 years can trigger a driver's license suspension.
  • At-fault accidents generally carry 0 DMV points unless paired with a moving citation.
  • A DMV driver improvement clinic can reduce license points (once every 5 years), but does not remove insurance points.
NC SDIP Insurance Points

Determines your policy rate surcharge

Governed by the NC Rate Bureau's Safe Driver Incentive Plan (SDIP) to calculate mandatory rate surcharges.

  • Even 1 insurance point increases your base rate by +40%; 2 points equals +55%; 4 points equals +90%.
  • Applies to both moving convictions and at-fault accidents above state damage thresholds.
  • Follows your policy for 3 years (minor incidents) or 5 years (4+ point convictions) under 2026 rules.
5 Years
Lookback for 4+ Point Tickets

Convictions of 4 or more SDIP points (such as reckless driving or passing a stopped school bus) now stay on your insurance record for 60 months, up from 36 months.

1 Per Home
PJC Waiver Every 5 Years

A Prayer for Judgment Continued (PJC) only waives insurance points for one ticket per household every 5 years. Using a second PJC can reactivate the first ticket's points.

$2,300
Accident Damage Threshold

At-fault property damage thresholds are indexed for modern repair costs: 1 pt up to $2,300, 2 pts from $2,301–$3,849, and 3 pts at $3,850 or more.

Two tickets in one traffic stop — how many points?

Under NC SDIP Rule 5.B.1.a, you are only charged insurance points for the single highest-point offense from the same stop. Check the "Same traffic stop" box in the calculator above to apply this rule automatically.

When does a minor speeding ticket get 0 insurance points?

In North Carolina, speeding 10 mph or less over the limit in a zone under 55 mph can qualify for an insurance point waiver if it did not happen in a school zone and you have had no other moving violations in the past 3 years.

Does a seatbelt or parking ticket add insurance points?

No. Seatbelt citations, parking tickets, and expired inspection/registration tickets are non-moving violations in North Carolina and add 0 DMV points and 0 insurance points.

Can an independent agent help if I already have points?

Yes. While SDIP surcharge percentages are standardized, base rates and underwriting discounts vary significantly between carriers like Progressive, National General, Travelers, and Nationwide. We compare multiple companies to find your most affordable option.
