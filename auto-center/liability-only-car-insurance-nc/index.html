The basics

What liability-only covers

Liability-only is the least car insurance North Carolina allows on a registered vehicle, which usually makes it the lowest-priced policy you can legally buy. It pays for injuries and damage you cause to other people. It does not pay to repair or replace your own car.

Own it outright?

Consider what your car is worth, and whether you could afford to repair or replace it.

  • The car is paid off, with no loan or lease.
  • It is older and you could replace it yourself.
  • It is a second vehicle that is rarely driven.
Check first

Still making payments?

Your lender may require comprehensive and collision. Check before changing coverage.

  • The car is financed or leased.
  • You depend on it to get to work.
  • It is newer or still worth a meaningful amount.

A simple test: if the car were totaled tomorrow and no one paid you for it, would you be all right? If yes, liability-only is a reasonable choice. If no, ask us to price collision and comprehensive with a higher deductible before you rule them out.

Your limits

State minimum or one step up

North Carolina's minimum is written as 50/100/50, and uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is written into the policy as well. Those limits took effect July 1, 2025. The minimum keeps you legal. It does not cap what you can owe if you cause a serious accident.

Injuries to one person$50,000$100,000

Straight answers

Questions about liability-only insurance

Is liability-only car insurance legal in North Carolina?+

Yes. Liability coverage at the state minimum of 50/100/50, with uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage, is all North Carolina requires to register and drive a car you own outright. Collision and comprehensive are optional unless a lender or leasing company requires them.

Does liability-only insurance pay to fix my own car?+

No. Liability pays for injuries and property damage you cause to other people. Damage to your own car from a crash you cause, a deer, hail, a fallen tree, fire or theft is paid only by collision and comprehensive coverage.

Can I carry liability-only on a financed or leased car?+

Almost never. Loan and lease contracts require collision and comprehensive until the vehicle is paid off. If you drop them, the lender can buy its own coverage and add the cost to your loan.

If I hit a deer with liability-only coverage, am I covered?+

Not for your own car. A deer strike is a comprehensive claim, and a liability-only policy has no comprehensive coverage. Injuries to your passengers may be covered if you bought medical payments coverage.

Do I still need insurance on a car I am not driving?+

Yes, as long as it has a North Carolina plate on it. The state requires continuous liability coverage on every registered vehicle. If you want to stop insuring a car, turn in the plate first, then cancel.

Is the state minimum enough coverage?+

It is enough to be legal. Whether it is enough to protect you depends on what you own and earn, because you are personally responsible for damages above your limits. We quote the minimum and one step higher side by side so you can see the difference before you decide.
