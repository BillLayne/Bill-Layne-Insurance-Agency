Understand your quote

What affects mobile home insurance costs in NC?

Your quote starts with your home and the protection you choose. These are four details to review with our team.

Your home and roof

Age, size, condition and roof details can affect pricing and which options are available. Tell us about recent updates and any additions.

Location and use

Your North Carolina address and how you use the home matter. Let us know if it is your main home, a seasonal home or rented to others.

Coverage choices

The amount of coverage, replacement cost or actual cash value settlement, and optional protections can change your quote. Compare these details alongside the premium.

Deductibles

A higher deductible may lower the premium but increases your share of a covered loss. Check whether a separate wind or hail deductible applies.

We compare top NC carriers to find your best available rate. Rates vary by carrier and underwriting.

FAQ

What else should NC mobile homeowners know?

Can you review an older mobile home?

Yes. We can review older single-wide and double-wide homes. Available options depend on the home’s age, condition, roof, location and use, as well as insurer requirements. A review does not guarantee a quote or coverage.

What information do I need for a quote?

Start with your home type, North Carolina ZIP code, name, phone number and preferred contact method. Your property address, approximate year built, roof details, how the home is used and current insurance help us complete the review. If photos or documents are needed, use our secure upload process.

What does replacement cost mean?

When included in your policy, replacement cost coverage generally pays to repair or replace covered property with materials of similar kind and quality without deducting for depreciation. Limits, deductibles, settlement conditions and policy terms apply. Actual cash value generally deducts depreciation. These amounts are different from a home’s market value.

Can I insure a rental mobile home?

Tell us if you rent the home to others or plan to do so. A rental home needs a review based on its use and may require different coverage from an owner-occupied home. Available options depend on the property, occupancy and insurer requirements.

Can I insure a mobile home on a rented lot?

Owning the home and renting the land are different from renting the home itself. If you own a mobile home on a leased lot or in a park, tell us about the location, occupancy and any park insurance requirements. We can review available options; eligibility and coverage depend on the home and insurer.

What’s the difference between manufactured and modular home insurance?

Manufactured homes are built to federal HUD standards, while modular homes are built to applicable state and local building codes. That distinction can affect the type of policy an insurer offers. Share the home’s year built and any available construction or identification documents so we can help review how it should be classified.

How are attached porches, decks and additions handled?

Tell us about attached porches, decks, carports and added rooms when requesting a quote or reviewing your policy. Ask how each structure is covered and whether its value is included in the coverage limit. Coverage depends on the policy, the construction and insurer requirements; do not assume an addition is automatically included.

What should I check about wind and hail coverage?

Ask whether wind and hail are covered at your address, whether exclusions apply, and whether there is a separate deductible. If the deductible is a percentage, ask which insured amount it uses and what that means in dollars. Also review how roof damage is valued, including any depreciation or roof settlement conditions.
