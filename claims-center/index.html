Photos, records & preparation

Organize the details. Take the next step.

A few clear records can help you explain what happened.

After an auto accident

  • Photograph damage and the surrounding scene, when safe.
  • Keep driver, insurance and witness details together.
  • Save the report number, estimates and receipts.
Start the Accident Helper →
View auto claim checklist

Record the date, location and a factual description of the incident. Include full-vehicle and close-up photos, and keep a copy of your communications with the adjuster. Ask your carrier how to send documents and whether an inspection is needed before repairs.

If anyone is injured or the scene is unsafe, get emergency help first.

After home or property damage

  • Photograph affected rooms and damaged belongings.
  • Make a list of items and gather available receipts.
  • Keep temporary repair invoices for carrier review.

A clearer path forward

What happens after you report a claim?

  1. 1

    Report the loss

    Contact your carrier and keep your claim number.

  2. 2

    Carrier review

    The adjuster gathers details and reviews your policy.

  3. 3

    Assessment & decision

    The carrier evaluates damage and explains its coverage decision.

  4. 4

    Resolution

    Covered claims may proceed to repairs or payment, subject to policy terms.

Timing and outcomes vary by loss, documentation and carrier review. Reporting a claim does not guarantee coverage or payment.

2025–2026 NC SDIP points & accident referenceView official table

North Carolina’s Safe Driver Incentive Plan (SDIP) sets statutory surcharges for at-fault accidents and moving convictions during the experience period. Not-at-fault accidents, comprehensive claims (hail, deer, theft, glass), and minor at-fault accidents meeting all safe-driver waiver criteria carry 0 SDIP points.

At-Fault Accident Point Thresholds & July 1, 2025 Rules

  • 1 SDIP Point (+40%): At-fault property damage only totaling $2,300 or less (if no minor-accident waiver applies).
  • 2 SDIP Points (+55%): At-fault property damage only between $2,301 and $3,849.
  • 3 SDIP Points (+70%): At-fault accident with property damage of $3,850 or more, or involving bodily injury/death.
  • July 1, 2025 NC Law Update (HB 382 / SB 319): Convictions on or after July 1, 2025, are subject to updated PJC and surcharge period rules, and drivers first licensed on or after July 1, 2025, face an expanded 8-year inexperienced operator structure.

Claims questions, explained

A few common questions.

What if the other driver does not have insurance?

Notify your carrier and ask it to review any uninsured motorist coverage that applies to your situation. Available coverage, limits and deductibles depend on your policy and the facts of the loss.

Should I accept the first settlement offer?

Ask the adjuster for a written explanation and compare it with your damage records and estimates. Discuss missing items or disagreements before accepting. Get legal advice if you have questions about signing a release or resolving an injury claim.

Will filing a claim affect my insurance rate?

It depends on fault, claim history and underwriting rules. North Carolina does not allow insurance points for an accident that was not your fault, but other underwriting factors can still affect your policy. Ask us or your carrier to review your situation.

View all claims questions
What is a Prayer for Judgment Continued (PJC) in NC?

A PJC is a court disposition. Its insurance effect depends on the offense, household driving history and the rules applicable to the conviction date. NC rules changed for convictions on or after July 1, 2025. Review the current NC Department of Insurance guidance and speak with an attorney about your case.

How long do I have to file a claim?

Notify your carrier promptly and follow your policy’s reporting requirements. A policy reporting deadline and the deadline for a lawsuit are different. Legal deadlines vary by claim and circumstances; ask an attorney about deadlines that may apply to you.

Does my insurance cover a rental car during repairs?

Rental reimbursement under your own policy generally requires the applicable coverage and is subject to limits and conditions. Ask the carrier about authorization, daily limits and duration before renting. A rental requested from another driver’s insurer is subject to its investigation and liability decision.

What is diminished value?

Diminished value refers to a possible reduction in a vehicle’s market value after damage and repair. Eligibility and the amount depend on the circumstances, evidence and applicable law. Ask the adjuster what documentation is needed; an attorney can advise you on your rights.

What should I do after storm damage to my home?

Prioritize safety, document damage and notify your carrier promptly. If safe, take reasonable temporary steps to prevent further damage and keep receipts. Ask the adjuster about inspections, damaged items and permanent repairs. Storm volume and the extent of damage can affect timing.
