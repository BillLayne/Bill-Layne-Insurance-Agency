NORTH CAROLINA POWERSPORTS FAQ

ATV & Side-by-Side insurance questions

Will my NC homeowners or auto policy cover my ATV or side-by-side?

Do not assume it will. Standard North Carolina homeowners (HO-3) policies generally exclude motor vehicles registered for road use and restrict off-road recreational vehicle liability to narrow situations on the insured residence premises—leaving trail riding, hunting-land trips, and physical damage to the machine itself uncovered. Standard personal auto policies also exclude four-wheelers and vehicles designed primarily for off-road use.

What insurance is required for a street-legal side-by-side (MUV) in North Carolina?

Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-121.1 and § 20-4.01(27), a qualifying four-wheel side-by-side registered as a Modified Utility Vehicle (MUV) with Form MVR-59 must carry continuous North Carolina motor vehicle liability insurance at minimum limits of 50/100/50 ($50,000 per person bodily injury, $100,000 per accident bodily injury, and $50,000 property damage) plus matching Uninsured/Underinsured Motorist coverage, verified with Form FS-1.

Where can you legally drive a plated Modified Utility Vehicle in North Carolina?

Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-121.1, a registered Modified Utility Vehicle may generally operate on North Carolina public streets and highways where the posted speed limit is 55 mph or less (or 35 mph or less on roads with four or more lanes, unless crossing an intersection), subject to any NCDOT or municipal safety restrictions. If the machine lacks both a windshield and windshield wipers, all occupants must wear a strapped DOT FMVSS 218 helmet.

Can I cancel insurance on a plated side-by-side during the winter?

Never cancel liability insurance while your Modified Utility Vehicle still has an active North Carolina license plate. Under N.C. Gen. Stat. § 20-309(d) and § 20-311, NCDMV tracks continuous liability electronically and assesses civil lapse penalties and registration revocations unless you surrender the license plate to NCDMV before ending coverage.

Are winches, roofs, light bars, and transport trailers covered?

Powersports policies that include comprehensive and collision coverage often provide a modest base allowance for permanently attached aftermarket equipment—commonly $1,000 to $3,000 depending on the carrier—and let you schedule higher limits for roofs, windshields, winches, light bars, audio systems, plows, and dedicated transport trailers.

What if I use my ATV or UTV for farm, property, or business work?

Always disclose how the vehicle is used before binding a policy. Personal recreational powersports policies often restrict commercial, fee-based, rental, or business operations, whereas private homestead chores, hunting-lease access, or farm utility use may require specific carrier eligibility or farm/commercial placement.
